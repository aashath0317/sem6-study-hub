## Why scan the environment?

- Marketing starts by **recognising customer needs and wants**, which means **analysing the business environment and spotting opportunities**. This is **environmental scanning**.
- **Effective marketing planning is only possible after proper environmental scanning.**

:::example Sri Lankan example
After the **2019 Easter Sunday attacks** in Sri Lanka, many businesses and industries (tourism, hotels, retail) suffered badly. Any factor with a **direct or indirect impact** shapes a business's future. These forces **change unexpectedly**, so **continuous monitoring and updating** is essential.
:::

:::key Definition
The **marketing environment** is the **internal and external factors and forces** that affect the market for a product.
:::

| Layer | What it is | Impact on the business |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | Factors **inside** the company | Controllable |
| **Micro** (task) environment | Actors **close** to the company | **Direct** |
| **Macro** environment | **Larger societal forces** | **Indirect**, but they still affect the market |

## Internal environment — 5 Ms & 7S

:::grid
**The 5 Ms**

- **Men** (people)
- **Money**
- **Machinery**
- **Material**
- **Market**
+++
**McKinsey's 7S model**

- **Strategy**
- **Structure**
- **Systems**
- **Style**
- **Staff**
- **Skills**
- **Shared values**
:::

Functional areas such as top management, manufacturing, finance and accounting are also part of the internal environment. **Changes in any of these directly affect product sales.**

## Micro environment (task environment)

There are **six groups** that **directly** affect the company:

:::flow
Company (internal)
Suppliers
Intermediaries
Customers
Competitors
Publics
:::

### Suppliers
- Provide the **resources** needed to produce goods and services. They are an important link in the **value delivery system**.
- They can **negotiate prices** and **bargain over terms**.
- **The number of suppliers** for the same material affects **industry attractiveness**.

### Marketing intermediaries
- Parties that **promote, sell and distribute** the product: **resellers, physical distribution firms, marketing service agencies, financial intermediaries**.
- Their own changes and objectives affect you directly.
- Modern marketers treat them as **partners**, not just channels.

### Customers
**Switching cost** is the cost to the customer of moving to a competitor. **High switching costs keep customers with you.**

| Customer market | Who buys | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Consumer** | Individuals and households | **Personal consumption** |
| **Business** | Organisations | **Further processing** or use in production |
| **Reseller** | Wholesalers and retailers | **Resell at a profit** |
| **Government** | Government agencies | Produce **public services** or transfer to those who need them |
| **International** | Buyers **in other countries** | Any of the above |

### Competitors
Firms offering **similar products to your target market**. The aim is a **strategic advantage** over them. Strategies depend on **size and industry position**:

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Market leader** | **Largest market share** and highest profit |
| **Market challenger** | **Always challenges** the leader |
| **Market follower** | **Copies the leader's strategies** |
| **Market nicher** | Serves a **focused niche** others ignore |

:::example Coca-Cola in Sri Lanka (the PDF's task)
- **Leader:** Coca-Cola
- **Challenger:** Pepsi; locally, Elephant House soft drinks
- **Followers:** smaller local cola brands copying the leader
- **Nichers:** speciality or health drinks such as king coconut water, craft sodas and energy drinks for a narrow segment
:::

### Publics (seven types)
A **public** is **any group with an actual or potential interest in, or impact on, the organisation's ability to achieve its objectives**.

| Public | Example |
|---|---|
| **Financial** | Banks and investors: affect the ability to **obtain funds** |
| **Media** | Newspapers, TV, online media: **news, features, editorial opinion** |
| **Government** | Regulate **public safety, truth in advertising** |
| **Citizen-action** | Consumer organisations, environmental groups, minority groups |
| **Local** | Neighbourhood residents, community organisations |
| **General** | The public's attitude towards the company's products and activities |
| **Internal** | Workers, managers, volunteers, board of directors |

## Macro environment (DENTPC)

The **external forces** with **no direct relationship** to the company that still **affect the market**. There are **six** of them.

| Force | Meaning | Trends to know |
|---|---|---|
| **D – Demographic** | **People make up markets**: age, gender, race, occupation, size, density, location | Changing family structure · geographic shifts · changing age structure · increased education · growing ethnic and cultural diversity |
| **E – Economic** | Factors affecting **purchasing power and spending patterns**: GDP, **income distribution**, interest rates | Inflation and recession · economic development · changing spending patterns · changes in income |
| **N – Natural** | **Natural resources** used as inputs or affected by marketing; climate, disasters | Shortages of raw materials · increased pollution · government intervention |
| **T – Technological** | Knowledge, innovation, research: creates **opportunities and threats** | Rapid pace of change · unlimited opportunities · increased regulation · practical, affordable products |
| **P – Political** | **Laws, government agencies, pressure groups** that influence or limit business. Good legislation **encourages competition and fair markets** | Increasing legislation to protect groups · changing enforcement · emphasis on **ethics and social responsibility** |
| **C – Cultural** | Institutions and forces shaping **basic values, perceptions, preferences, behaviours**, passed from parents and reinforced by schools, religion, business and government | Values about **oneself, others, organisations, society, nature, the universe** |

:::tip Memory hook: "DENTPC"
**D**emographic · **E**conomic · **N**atural · **T**echnological · **P**olitical · **C**ultural. Compare with PESTEL, which uses similar ideas.
:::

## Responding to the environment

:::grid
**🐢 Reactive approach**

Act on an opportunity or threat **only after the change occurs**. Treat the environment as **uncontrollable**: watch and adapt.
+++
**🚀 Proactive approach**

Believe environmental forces **can be influenced or controlled** in the firm's favour, using **political, psychological and public-relations skills**:

- hire **lobbyists** to influence legislation
- stage **media events** for good press
- file **lawsuits and complaints** to keep competitors in line
- form **contracts** to control distribution channels
:::

## Marketing information system (MIS) & planning

:::key Marketing information system
The **people, equipment and procedures** that **gather, sort, analyse, evaluate and distribute needed, timely and accurate information** to marketing decision makers.
:::

It draws on:

- **Internal records**: sales, costs, inventory, orders, receivables, customer databases.
- **External sources**: marketing intelligence (competitors, trends) and marketing research.

Environmental scanning, supported by the MIS, **feeds marketing planning**: setting objectives, choosing target markets and designing the marketing mix based on the opportunities and threats found.
