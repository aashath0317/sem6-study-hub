## The trading account → gross profit

The **trading account** calculates the **gross profit** earned from **buying and selling goods** in a period (usually one year).

:::formula
**Cost of sales = Opening inventory + Purchases (+ carriage inwards, import duty − purchase returns) − Closing inventory**
**Gross profit = Sales (net of sales returns) − Cost of sales**
:::

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Opening stock** | Stock left over from **last year**. It's the first to be sold this year |
| **Purchases** | Cost of goods **bought** this year. **Add carriage inwards and import duty**; **deduct purchase returns (returns outwards)** |
| **Closing stock** | Goods **left at year end**. They're **deducted** because they weren't sold this year |
| **Gross profit** | The profit from **buying and selling** |

**Format: trading account for the year ended 31 March 20xx**

| | Rs | Rs |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Sales** | | xxxx |
| Less: sales returns | | (xx) |

:::warn Carriage inwards vs carriage outwards
- **Carriage inwards** (on goods **bought**) is part of **cost of sales**.
- **Carriage outwards** (delivering goods **to customers**) is a **distribution expense**.
:::

## The income statement → net profit

The **comprehensive income statement** calculates the **net profit** after **all expenses** for the period. Start from **gross profit**, add **other income** (rent received, discount received, interest received), and deduct expenses grouped as:

| Expense group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Administration expenses** | Costs of **running** the business: office **wages, insurance, electricity**, rent, stationery, depreciation of office equipment |
| **Distribution (selling) expenses** | Costs of **selling and delivering**: **advertising, delivery van repairs and petrol**, carriage outwards, sales commission, **discount allowed** |
| **Finance expenses** | Costs of **borrowing**: **overdraft interest, mortgage or loan interest** |

## LKAS 1 / IAS 1 — Presentation of Financial Statements

**Objective:** prescribe the **basis for presenting general-purpose financial statements**, so they're **comparable** with the entity's previous periods **and** with other entities. It sets the **overall requirements, structure guidelines and minimum content**.

- **Scope:** all general-purpose statements prepared under IFRS, for users who **can't demand tailored reports**.
- **The objective of financial statements:** information about **financial position, performance and cash flows**, useful for economic decisions.
  - They cover **assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses (including gains and losses), owner contributions and distributions, and cash flows**.
  - This helps users **predict future cash flows**, especially their **timing and certainty**.

### A complete set of financial statements [[00:01]]

1. **Statement of financial position** (balance sheet) at the end of the period
2. **Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income**, as one statement or two
3. **Statement of changes in equity**
4. **Statement of cash flows**
5. **Notes**: significant accounting policies and explanatory notes
6. **Comparative information**

Plus a **third balance sheet** (at the start of the earliest comparative period) after a **retrospective** policy change, restatement or reclassification. All statements get **equal prominence**; other titles may be used.

### General features

| Principle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Fair presentation and compliance** | Statements must **"present fairly"**: **faithful representation** under the Framework's definitions. Complying with IFRS (plus disclosure) is presumed fair. An **explicit, unreserved statement of compliance** goes in the notes |
| **Going concern** | Management must **assess** it. **Disclose significant uncertainties**. If the entity is not a going concern, **don't use the going-concern basis** and disclose this |
| **Accrual basis** | Prepare using **accruals**, **except cash-flow information** |
| **Consistency of presentation** | Keep presentation and classification from **period to period**, unless circumstances change or a new IFRS requires it |
| **Materiality and aggregation** | Information is **material** if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably **influence users' decisions**. Present each **material class separately**; aggregate dissimilar items only if **immaterial** |
| **Offsetting** | **Don't offset** assets and liabilities, or income and expenses, **unless an IFRS requires or permits it** |
| **Comparative information** | Disclose **prior-period** comparatives for **all amounts**, including relevant narrative. At least **two of each primary statement** [[04:16]] |
| **Identification** | Clearly identify the statements and notes. Show the **entity name, single or group entity, reporting period, presentation currency, rounding level** |
| **Reporting period** | **At least annually**. If it changes, disclose why and that amounts aren't fully comparable |

### Current vs non-current [[00:34]]

| | **Current** | **Non-current** |
|---|---|---|
| **Assets** | Realised in the **normal operating cycle**; held for **trading**; realised **within 12 months**; **cash and cash equivalents** (unless restricted) | All others |
| **Liabilities** | Settled in the **normal operating cycle**; held for trading; due **within 12 months**; **no unconditional right to defer** beyond 12 months | All others |

:::video Exam tip from the lecture [[00:34]]
**Split items that straddle 12 months.** A loan's instalment **due this year is current** and the **rest is non-current**. The same applies to **leases**. Receivables are current.
:::

- **Format is not prescribed.** Assets can go current-first or non-current-first. A **net-assets** presentation and the UK "long-term financing" format are also acceptable.
- A **classified** (current / non-current) balance sheet is normal, unless a **liquidity-based** presentation is more reliable.

### Profit or loss vs other comprehensive income [[01:31]]

:::formula
**Total comprehensive income = Profit or loss + Other comprehensive income (OCI)**
:::

- **Profit or loss** = total income − expenses, **excluding OCI items**.
- **OCI** = income and expense items **not recognised in profit or loss** because another IFRS says so. *Examples:* **revaluation of non-current assets, foreign exchange translation, certain pension movements**.
- **Total comprehensive income** = the **change in equity** in a period **other than from transactions with owners**.
- It can be presented as **one combined statement** or **two separate statements** [[02:03]].

:::warn No "exceptional items" [[03:40]]
IAS 1 **doesn't use the term "exceptional items"**. It requires **separate disclosure** of material items such as **restructuring costs and impairment losses**, either on the face of the statements or in the notes.
:::

## Beginner practice — questions and step-by-step answers

**Week 05 learning path:** Week 05: Trading account → Income statement.

**Start from zero:** Sales revenue is earned from customers. Cost of sales is the cost of goods actually sold. Gross profit is sales minus cost of sales; net profit also deducts operating expenses.

These are **new beginner learning questions**, not official past-paper questions or a replacement for the course-paper practice above. All amounts are in Sri Lankan rupees (Rs) unless stated otherwise.

**How to practise:** read the question, write your own attempt, then open the worked answer. Follow the reason for each step. Use the final check to find mistakes; mark this section understood only when you can explain the method.

### Practice 05.1 — Prepare gross and net profit

**Question:** Opening inventory Rs 10,000; purchases Rs 50,000; purchase returns Rs 2,000; carriage inwards Rs 3,000; closing inventory Rs 11,000; sales Rs 90,000; sales returns Rs 5,000; rent Rs 8,000; wages Rs 7,000. Wages are operating expenses here. All figures are Rs; no other items or adjustments.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 05.1</summary>

1. **Net sales** = 90,000 − 5,000 = **85,000**. Customers’ returns reduce revenue.
2. **Net purchases** = 50,000 − 2,000 = **48,000**. Returns to suppliers reduce purchases.
3. **Goods available for sale** = opening inventory 10,000 + net purchases 48,000 + carriage inwards 3,000 = **61,000**.
4. **Cost of sales** = 61,000 − closing inventory 11,000 = **50,000**. Unsold goods are an asset, not this period’s cost of sales.
5. **Gross profit** = 85,000 − 50,000 = **35,000**.
6. **Operating expenses** = rent 8,000 + wages 7,000 = **15,000**.
7. **Net profit** = 35,000 − 15,000 = **Rs 20,000**.

**Answer format:** Net sales 85,000; less cost of sales 50,000; gross profit 35,000; less operating expenses 15,000; net profit 20,000.

**Check:** purchases are not automatically equal to cost of sales. Always account for opening and closing inventory.

</details>

### Practice 05.2 — Separate owner transactions from profit

**Question:** The owner adds Rs 20,000 capital, takes Rs 3,000 cash drawings, and the business earns Rs 15,000 service revenue and incurs Rs 6,000 expenses. Find profit and the change in owner equity.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 05.2</summary>

1. Income and expenses determine profit: 15,000 − 6,000 = **Rs 9,000**.
2. Capital introduced is an owner contribution, not revenue.
3. Drawings are a withdrawal of equity, not a business operating expense.
4. Equity increases by contribution 20,000 + profit 9,000 − drawings 3,000 = **Rs 26,000**.

**Check:** profit is 9,000 while the increase in equity is 26,000. They answer different questions.

</details>
