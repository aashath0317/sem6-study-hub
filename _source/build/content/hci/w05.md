## Action, interaction, effect, feedback

These four words are easy to mix up:

| Term | Meaning | Lecture example: the lecturer talking |
|---|---|---|
| **Action** | Something one party does | The lecturer **talks** |
| **Interaction** | **The effect of an action** carried out between **two or more objects**, causing **effects on one another** [[v2 00:51]] | Students hear it and process it (senses → perception → cognition) |
| **Effect** | The change caused by the action | Students learn something. The lecturer is affected too: they keep wondering whether students understand [[v2 03:36]] |
| **Feedback** | A **response that informs the actor about the outcome** of their action [[v2 00:00]] | **Missing**: mics muted, cameras off, chat unread, so there's **no synchronous feedback** [[v2 04:51]] |

:::key Why feedback matters [[v2 05:40]]
"If I see what has happened to you based on my action, then I know exactly whether **my intention was successful**." Without feedback, the actor can't tell whether the action worked.
:::

:::warn Effect ≠ feedback [[03:43]]
The lecturer insists you separate them:

- **Pressing volume-up** is the *interaction*.
- **The volume increasing** is the *effect*.
- **Hearing the louder sound** is the *feedback*.

Feedback is **information about the effect**, not the effect itself [[00:37]].
:::

:::key Adaptive learning principle [[06:39]]
**People learn through feedback cycles.** Act → observe the consequence → evaluate → adjust future behaviour. A **success** message confirms your actions were right. An **error** message also teaches you that something was wrong. Either way, **the next interaction improves** [[07:17]].
:::

*Ubiquitous computing* (sensors + hardware + ML/AI, everywhere and almost invisible) is a special area of HCI and human-centred computing [[06:09]].

## Norman's model revisited: the two bridges

Mapping Norman's 7 stages between the **user's goal** and the **world** (the physical system) gives **two bridges**:

<figure><img src="Figure%205.1.PNG" alt="Bridges of execution and evaluation"><figcaption>Figure 5.1: The bridges of execution and evaluation</figcaption></figure>

:::grid
**🌉 Bridge of Execution** (goal → world)

1. **Plan**: form a strategy for the goal
2. **Specify**: map it to concrete interface actions
3. **Perform**: physically operate the controls
+++
**🔍 Bridge of Evaluation** (world → goal)

1. **Perceive**: sense the status change
2. **Interpret**: work out what the feedback means
3. **Compare**: check the outcome against the goal
:::

The bridges are how the **gulfs of execution and evaluation** (Week 2) get crossed.

## The 7 questions — designer & user perspectives

The **same 7 stages** become questions. **Designers** use them to **audit** an interface; **users** ask them (subconsciously) while interacting [[v4 02:52]].

| Stage | 🛠 **Designer asks** (how easily can the user…) | 🙋 **User asks** |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Goal** | …**determine the function** of the device? | "**What do I want to accomplish?**" |
| 2. **Plan** | …**tell what actions are possible**? | "**What are the alternatives?**" |
| 3. **Specify** | …**map intention to physical controls** (natural mapping)? | "**What can I do now?**" |
| 4. **Execute** | …**perform** the action (click, swipe, press)? | "**How do I do it?**" |
| 5. **Perceive** | …**perceive the state change** (clear feedback)? | "**What happened?**" |
| 6. **Interpret** | …**understand what the state means**? | "**What does it mean?**" |
| 7. **Evaluate** | …**confirm the state meets the goal**? | "**Is this okay? Have I done what I set out to do?**" |

Stages 1–4 are the **execution phase**; stages 5–7 are the **evaluation phase**.

## Norman's three levels of design

Norman maps the action cycle onto **three levels of cognitive processing** [[v5 00:02]]:

<figure><img src="Figure%205.4%20(1).PNG" alt="Three levels of design"><figcaption>Figure 5.4: The three levels mapped to the action cycle</figcaption></figure>

| Level | Nature | Governs (action cycle) | Design concerns | Example: a smartphone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 👁️ **Visceral** | **Immediate, subconscious** sensory response; **closest to physical action and senses** [[v5 00:36]] | **Execution** (performing) and **perception** | **First impressions**: look, beauty, feel, sound | Sleek glass, colour, the satisfying click |
| ⚙️ **Behavioural** | **Practical** use, **interpretative** [[v5 01:48]] | **Action specification** and **interpretation** | **How well it works**: usability, responsiveness, ergonomic efficiency | Fast unlock, smooth scrolling, easy camera |
| 👑 **Reflective** | **Conscious, highest-level thought**; reflecting on what was received | **Goal** and **final evaluation** | **Meaning**: self-image, **pride of ownership**, **brand loyalty**, satisfaction looking back | "I'm proud to own this brand", memories |

:::video How the levels interact [[v5 02:31]]
**Sensory input** first reaches the **visceral** level and also the **behavioural** level. The behavioural level combines visceral and reflective input to **interpret**. The **reflective** level is pure thought and is **not directly bound** to sensory input or motor output [[v5 03:59]]. **Motor output** is driven through the **behavioural and visceral** levels together.
:::

### Reframed as depth of thinking

:::flow
⚡ Reaction (visceral): reflexes, immediate capture
🤔 Deliberation (behavioural): forming actions, decoding feedback
🧠 Metacognition (reflective): thinking about one's own thinking
:::

| Tier | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest | **Reaction** (visceral) | Subconscious reflexes and immediate sensory capture tied to the physical world |
| Middle | **Deliberation** (behavioural) | Forming specific action sequences and decoding feedback to finish the task |
| Top | **Metacognition** (reflective) | **Thinking about one's own thinking**: judging strategies, solving new edge cases, emotional fulfilment |

:::tip Balanced feedback
Feedback should **clearly inform without being distracting, noisy or intrusive**.
:::

## Quick revision sheet

| Concept | Remember it as |
|---|---|
| Interaction | Effect of an action between 2+ objects, affecting one another |
| Feedback | Information about the effect, returned to the actor |
| Effect vs feedback | Volume increases (effect) vs you hear it louder (feedback) |
| Examples | Kettle whistle · volume · bank "success/failed" · ATM · robots |
| Why | We learn through feedback cycles, so the next interaction improves |
| Bridge of execution | Plan → Specify → Perform |
| Bridge of evaluation | Perceive → Interpret → Compare |
| Designer's 7 Qs | How easily can the user determine function / tell actions / map / perform / perceive / interpret / evaluate |
| User's 7 Qs | What do I want? Alternatives? What can I do? How? What happened? What does it mean? Is it okay? |
| Visceral | Look and feel; reaction |
| Behavioural | Usability; deliberation |
| Reflective | Meaning, pride, brand; metacognition |
