| Q | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Provides historical financial data for external users** | Financial accounting reports what already happened (historical) to outsiders such as investors, banks, government and suppliers. |
| 2 | **Only when cash is received from customers** | Cash basis: record income only when the money comes in. (Accrual basis: record it when it is earned.) |
| 3 | **Cash** | Cash is an asset the business owns, so it is a real account. Real = assets; nominal = expenses, losses, income, gains. |
| 4 | **Costs are recorded in the same period as the revenues they help generate** | Matching: put each expense in the same period as the revenue it helped earn, so the profit for that period is correct. |
| 5 | **Serve as evidence of transactions** | A source document is the proof (evidence) that a transaction happened. It is the first step of the accounting process. |
| 6 | **Revenues and expenses are recognized when they occur** | Accrual concept: income when earned and expenses when incurred, whether or not cash has moved. |
| 7 | **Classifying and summarizing journal entries into accounts** | The ledger takes the journal entries and sorts them into accounts (Cash a/c, Sales a/c …), giving each account's balance. |
| 8 | **Revenue** | Revenue is income, so it is a nominal account. Tip: real = things, personal = people, nominal = income and expenses. |
| 9 | **Each transaction affects at least two accounts** | Every transaction has a debit in one account and an equal credit in another, so total debits always equal total credits. |
| 10 | **Debit Cash, Credit Sales** | Cash a/c Dr 6,000,000; Sales a/c Cr 6,000,000. Asset up = debit, income up = credit. |
| 11 | **Interest paid on bank loans** | Interest paid is commonly shown under operating activities (LKAS 7 also allows financing). The other three are clearly investing or financing, so this is the best answer. |
| 12 | **Repayment of a bank loan** | Repaying a loan is money going back to a lender, so it is a financing outflow. |
| 13 | **Possible losses are recorded immediately** | Prudence: record expected losses as soon as they are foreseen, but record gains only when they actually happen. |
| 14 | **It summarizes transactions account-wise after they are journalized** | After journalising, entries are posted to the ledger, which groups them account by account. |
| 15 | **Building** | A building is kept and used for many years, so it is a non-current asset (property, plant and equipment). |
| 16 | **Providing evidence for business transactions** | Source documents (invoice, receipt, cheque counterfoil, debit/credit note) are the evidence for each entry. |
| 17 | **The fund is replenished to a fixed amount periodically** | Imprest: start with a fixed float (e.g. Rs. 5,000). At the end of each period the cashier is given exactly what was spent, bringing the float back to the fixed amount. |
| 18 | **Debit Purchases, Credit Accounts Payable** | Purchases a/c Dr 550,000; Accounts payable (creditor) a/c Cr 550,000. It goes in the purchases journal. |
| 19 | **Recording transactions that do not fit into special journals** | The general journal records the unusual entries: opening entries, credit purchase of fixed assets, corrections, write-offs, adjustments. |
| 20 | **Profitability** | Profitability is about how well the business performs, not about the quality of the information. |
