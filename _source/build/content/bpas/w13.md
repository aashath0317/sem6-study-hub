## What is project appraisal?

:::key Definition
**Project appraisal** is the process of **assessing and questioning proposals before resources are committed**. It evaluates **whether an investment project is worthwhile**.
:::

**Key issues of project appraisal:**

- **environmental factors**
- **options** (alternatives)
- **inputs**
- **value for money**
- **implementation**
- **risk and uncertainty**

| **Non-discounting methods** (ignore the time value of money) | **Discounting methods** (consider the time value of money) |
|---|---|
| **Payback period** | **Net present value (NPV)** |
| **Accounting rate of return (ARR)** | **Internal rate of return (IRR)** |

## Non-discounting methods

### Payback period

:::key Definition
**Payback** is the length of time taken to **repay the initial capital cost** from the project's cash inflows [[v1 06:27]].
:::

- **Equal cash flows:** payback = **initial investment ÷ annual cash inflow**.
- **Unequal cash flows:** **accumulate** the cash flows year by year until the cost is recovered. Then add the fraction of the year: **(amount still needed ÷ that year's cash flow)**.
- **Decision:** accept if payback ≤ the **benchmark** period. Among mutually exclusive projects, choose the **shortest** payback.

:::example Projects A and B [[v1 08:07]]
| Year | Project A | Cumulative A | Project B | Cumulative B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | (900,000) | (900,000) | (1,000,000) | (1,000,000) |
| 1 | 700,000 | (200,000) | 600,000 | (400,000) |
| 2 | 100,000 | (100,000) | 400,000 | **0** |
| 3 | 100,000 | **0** | 400,000 | 400,000 |
| 4 | 1,300,000 | 1,300,000 | 400,000 | 800,000 |

**Payback:** A = **3 years**, B = **2 years**, so **choose B** [[v1 12:51]].

**Notice:** payback **ignores A's huge 1.3m in year 4**. That's its main weakness [[v1 15:08]].
:::

| **Advantages** [[v1 13:31]] | **Disadvantages** [[v1 14:38]] |
|---|---|
| **Easy to calculate and interpret** | **Ignores cash flows after payback** |
| Favours **quick liquidity**; less risk | The benchmark period is **arbitrary** [[v1 16:05]] |
| **Facilitates further evaluation**: a first screen before NPV and IRR | **Ignores the time value of money** [[v1 17:59]] |

### Discounted payback period
This fixes the time-value problem: **discount each cash flow first**, then do payback on the **discounted** figures [[v1 18:56]].

:::example Same projects, discount rate 20% [[v1 19:38]]
| Year | DF = 1/1.2ⁿ | A: PV | A: cumulative | B: PV | B: cumulative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.8333 | 583,333 | 583,333 | 500,000 | 500,000 |
| 2 | 0.6944 | 69,444 | 652,778 | 277,778 | 777,778 |
| 3 | 0.5787 | 57,870 | 710,648 | 231,481 | 1,009,259 |
| 4 | 0.4823 | 626,929 | 1,337,577 | 192,901 | |

- **A** = 3 + (900,000 − 710,648) ÷ 626,929 = **3.30 years**
- **B** = 2 + (1,000,000 − 777,778) ÷ 231,481 = **2.96 years**

**Choose B** [[v1 27:14]].
:::

### Accounting rate of return (ARR)

:::formula
**ARR = Average annual profit ÷ Investment × 100%**
PDF: ÷ **initial cost**.   Video: ÷ **average investment = (cost + scrap) ÷ 2**
:::

- ARR compares **accounting profit** (after depreciation and tax), **not cash flows**, with the investment [[v1 27:43]].
- **Decision:** accept if ARR ≥ the **predetermined minimum ARR (benchmark)**; choose the **highest** ARR [[v1 30:06]].

:::example $400,000 machine, 4 years, no scrap, benchmark 20% [[v1 32:28]]
| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Profit after 15% tax | 51,000 | 68,000 | 59,500 | 76,500 | 255,000 |

- **Average profit** = 255,000 ÷ 4 = **63,750**
- **Average investment** = (400,000 + 0) ÷ 2 = **200,000**
- **ARR** = 63,750 ÷ 200,000 = **31.875%**. That's above 20%, so **invest** [[v1 35:41]].

**On initial cost** (the PDF formula), ARR = 63,750 ÷ 400,000 = 15.9%. **State which base you use.**
:::

| **Advantages** | **Disadvantages** |
|---|---|
| Easy to compute and understand | **Ignores the time value of money** |
| Uses averages, so projects with **different lives can be compared** [[v1 36:31]] | **Ignores cash flows**: profit can be **manipulated** [[v1 37:14]] |

## Time value of money

:::key Definition
**Money available today is worth more than the same amount in the future**, because of its earning potential: money can earn interest and grow.
:::

**Three reasons** [[v2 04:48]]:

1. **Opportunity cost:** money in hand could earn a return elsewhere, such as a fixed deposit. Waiting means **giving up the best alternative** [[v2 05:29]].
2. **Inflation:** prices rise, so the **purchasing power of money falls**. Rs 1,000 buys fewer loaves next year [[v2 07:48]].
3. **Uncertainty and risk:** the future is uncertain (nobody predicted COVID in 2019), so investors demand a **risk premium** [[v2 09:47]].

:::formula
**Present value: PV = FV ÷ (1 + i)ⁿ**   i = interest (discount) rate, n = number of years
**Discount factor = 1 ÷ (1 + i)ⁿ**
:::

:::example Equal in today's money [[v2 13:37]]
At **10%**: receive **3,000 today**, or receive **1,100 (year 1) + 1,210 (year 2) + 1,331 (year 3)**?

| Year | Cash | ÷ (1.1)ⁿ | PV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1,100 | 1.100 | 1,000 |
| 2 | 1,210 | 1.210 | 1,000 |
| 3 | 1,331 | 1.331 | 1,000 |
| | | **Total** | **3,000** |

The two options have **the same present value**. Likewise, $110 in year 1 and $121 in year 2 are **both worth $100 today** [[v2 02:25]].
:::

## Net present value (NPV)

**Steps** [[v2 16:25]]:

1. Identify the **discount rate** (cost of capital).
2. Identify the project's **cash flows**.
3. **Discount** each cash flow to present value.
4. **Add** them and **deduct the initial investment**.

:::formula
**NPV = Σ CFₜ ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ − Initial investment**
:::

| NPV | Decision [[v2 17:45]] |
|---|---|
| **> 0** | **Accept**: the return beats an equal-risk alternative |
| **< 0** | **Reject** |
| **= 0** | **Indifferent**: accept if the project is necessary |
| Several projects with limited funds | Choose the **highest NPV** |

:::example Four projects at 12% [[v2 19:41]]
| Project | Initial investment | NPV | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| A | 900,000 (cash flows 120k, 250k, 400k, 1.3m) | **+517,327** | Accept |
| B | 1,000,000 | **+214,920** | Accept |
| C | 303,730 | **0** | Indifferent |
| D | 1,500,000 | **negative** | **Reject** |

**Resources for only one project:** choose **A**, the highest NPV [[v2 24:33]].

**Check for A:**

| Year | Working | PV |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | 120,000 ÷ 1.12 | 107,143 |
| 2 | 250,000 ÷ 1.2544 | 199,298 |
| 3 | 400,000 ÷ 1.4049 | 284,712 |
| 4 | 1,300,000 ÷ 1.5735 | 826,174 |
| | **Total PV** | **1,417,327** |
| | Less investment | (900,000) |
| | **NPV** | **517,327** ✓ |
:::

**Advantages of NPV** [[v2 24:56]]:

- it uses the **time value of money**
- it considers **all cash flows** over the project's life
- it uses **cash flows, not accounting profit** (which can be manipulated)
- it's consistent with **maximising shareholder wealth**

## Internal rate of return (IRR)

:::key Definition
**IRR** is the **discount rate that makes NPV = 0**: discounted inflows exactly equal discounted outflows [[v2 26:22]].

**Accept** if IRR ≥ the **cost of capital (benchmark)**; **reject** if it's lower.

- IRR helps measure the worth of an investment against **internal standards of return**.
- It allows comparison of projects with **different initial outlays**.
:::

**Calculating IRR (trial and interpolation)** [[v2 27:44]]:

1. Find the NPV at a rate that gives a **positive** NPV (P).
2. Find it at a higher rate that gives a **negative** NPV (N).
3. Interpolate:

:::formula
**IRR = L + [P ÷ (P − N)] × (H − L)**
L = lower rate, H = higher rate, P = NPV at L (positive), N = NPV at H (negative)
:::

:::example IRR example [[v2 30:26]]
Cost **$400,000**; inflows **$200,000 a year for 3 years**; benchmark **15%**.

| Rate | Annuity factor (3 years) | PV of inflows | NPV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 22% | 2.0422 | 408,440 | **+8.4k** (P) |
| 24% | 1.9813 | 396,260 | **−3.8k** (N) |

IRR = 22 + [8.4 ÷ (8.4 − (−3.8))] × (24 − 22) = 22 + (8.4 ÷ 12.2) × 2 = **23.38%** [[v2 32:24]]

Since 23.38% > 15%, **accept**.

**Watch the signs:** P − N = 8.4 + 3.8.
:::

**IRR disadvantages:**

- **complex**
- **time-consuming**
- needs **expertise to calculate and interpret** [[v2 32:45]]

## Which method is best?

| Method | Time value of money? | All cash flows? | Basis | Verdict |
|---|:-:|:-:|---|---|
| Payback | ✗ | ✗ (ignores later flows) | Cash | Simple, liquidity-focused |
| ARR | ✗ | ✓ | **Accounting profit** | Simple; profit can be manipulated |
| **NPV** | ✓ | ✓ | Cash | **Best**: wealth in absolute Rs [[v2 33:38]] |
| IRR | ✓ | ✓ | Cash | Good, but complex; gives a % |

:::exam
**Calculate:**

- **payback**, especially with unequal flows
- **ARR**
- **NPV** using discount factors
- **IRR** by interpolation (watch the minus sign)

**Then:**

- state the **accept or reject** decision and choose between projects
- **explain the time value of money** (opportunity cost, inflation, risk)
- give the **pros and cons** of each method, and **why NPV is best**
:::
