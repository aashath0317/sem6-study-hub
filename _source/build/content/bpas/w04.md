## Capital vs revenue expenditure

Spending on fixed assets is **either capital or revenue** expenditure. The distinction matters because **only capital expenditure is included in the cost of the fixed asset**. Revenue expenditure is an **expense** in the income statement.

| | **Capital expenditure** | **Revenue expenditure** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Costs of **acquiring** a fixed asset, plus later spending that **increases its earning capacity** | Costs that **maintain** (rather than enhance) the asset's earning capacity |
| **Includes** | **Purchase cost, delivery, legal charges, installation, upgrades, replacements**: all costs of bringing the asset to its **present location and condition** | **Repairs, maintenance, repainting, renewals** |
| **Frequency** | Usually **one-off** | **Regular, recurring** |
| **Benefit period** | **Several accounting periods** | A **relatively short** period |
| **Accounting** | **Added to the asset's cost** (statement of financial position), then depreciated | **Expensed** in the income statement in the period incurred |

:::example The biscuit machine
A company buys a machine to make biscuits.

- **Purchase and installation** = **capital** expenditure.
- **Later repairs and maintenance** = **revenue** expenditure. They only **maintain** the machine; they don't increase what it can earn.
:::

:::tip Quick test
Ask: **does it create or improve an asset** (more capacity, longer life, new capability), or is it the **cost of acquiring and installing** it? Then it's **capital**. Does it just **keep the asset running as before**? Then it's **revenue**.
:::

| Item | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Buying a van | **Capital** | Acquisition |
| Petrol for the van | Revenue | Running cost |
| Repairs to the van | Revenue | Maintains |
| Extra headlights on the van | **Capital** | Improvement |
| Rs 1,500 on a machine: Rs 1,000 improvement + Rs 500 repairs | **1,000 capital + 500 revenue** | Split by nature |
| Painting the outside of a **new** building | **Capital** | Part of getting the new asset ready for use |
| Altering a new van's interior to increase capacity | **Capital** | Increases earning capacity |
| Vehicle revenue licence for an existing van | Revenue | Recurring running cost |
| Cost of installing a new machine | **Capital** | Installation |
| Hiring a refrigeration plant | Revenue | Hire is not ownership |
| A car bought **for resale** by a motor dealer | Revenue (inventory / purchases) | It's trading stock, not a fixed asset |
| Acquiring patent rights | **Capital** | An intangible asset |
| Legal fees for a factory extension | **Capital** | Cost of the extension |

## The general ledger

:::key Definition
The **general ledger** is the **complete record of financial transactions and events**. It holds the account information needed for the financial statements, with accounts for **assets, liabilities, capital, income and expenses**.
:::

**Modern classification of ledger accounts:**

- **Asset accounts**: land, building, machinery, debtors, cash, bank
- **Liability accounts**: creditors, bank loan
- **Capital account**: capital, drawings
- **Income accounts**: sales, rent income, discount received
- **Expense accounts**: purchases, rent, wages, discount allowed

### Posting from the primary books
- **Day-book totals** go to the main accounts (e.g. sales journal total → **Cr Sales**). **Individual lines** go to personal accounts (each debtor **Dr**).
- **Cash-book** entries go to the **opposite** side of the other account. A receipt on the cash book's debit side is **credited** to the paying account.
- **General journal** entries are posted as stated.

## The trial balance

:::key Definition
A **trial balance** is a **list of the closing balances of ledger accounts on a certain date**. It is the **first step towards preparing financial statements**.

- **Debit balances:** assets, expenses, drawings.
- **Credit balances:** liabilities, capital, income.
- If everything is recorded and extracted correctly, **total debits = total credits**.
:::

### Purposes
1. A **working paper** for preparing **financial statements**.
2. Confirms that **every debit has a matching credit** (double entry).
3. Confirms balances were **accurately extracted** from the ledger.
4. Helps **identify and correct errors** *before* the statements are prepared. Fixing errors afterwards takes much longer.

### Limitations
- It **only confirms that total debits equal total credits**.
- **It can agree despite errors.** An incorrect debit can be offset by an equal incorrect credit. A transaction **never recorded at all** leaves both sides missing, so the totals still agree.

|---|---|
| Capital expenditure | Acquire or enhance an asset (purchase, delivery, legal, installation, upgrade, replacement); capitalised |
| Revenue expenditure | Maintain (repairs, maintenance, repainting, renewals); expensed |
| General ledger | Complete record; asset, liability, capital, income, expense accounts |
| Posting | Day-book totals to main accounts; individual lines to personal accounts; cash book entries to the opposite side |

### Practice 04.1 — Build a trial balance from scratch

**Question:** A new business receives Rs 30,000 owner capital in cash, buys equipment for Rs 10,000 cash, earns Rs 6,000 cash service revenue and pays Rs 2,000 rent. Prepare a trial balance. Ignore depreciation for this short example.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 04.1</summary>

1. Post the entries: Dr Cash / Cr Capital 30,000; Dr Equipment / Cr Cash 10,000; Dr Cash / Cr Service revenue 6,000; Dr Rent / Cr Cash 2,000.
2. Balance cash: 30,000 − 10,000 + 6,000 − 2,000 = **Rs 24,000 debit**.
3. List each remaining balance on its correct side.

| Account | Debit Rs | Credit Rs |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cash | 24,000 | — |
| Equipment | 10,000 | — |
| Rent expense | 2,000 | — |
| Capital | — | 30,000 |
| Service revenue | — | 6,000 |
| **Total** | **36,000** | **36,000** |

**Check:** profit = 6,000 − 2,000 = 4,000. Assets 34,000 equal capital 30,000 + profit 4,000. Trial-balance totals differ from total assets because expenses and revenue are also listed.

</details>

### Practice 04.2 — Classify costs and spot a hidden error

**Question:** A machine costs Rs 40,000, delivery to the factory costs Rs 2,000 and necessary installation costs Rs 3,000. Later routine servicing costs Rs 1,000. A clerk debits Repairs instead of Equipment for the machine’s Rs 40,000 purchase. Explain the correct treatment and whether the trial balance detects that error.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 04.2</summary>

1. Costs necessary to bring the machine to its location and working condition are capitalised: 40,000 + 2,000 + 3,000 = **Rs 45,000 equipment cost**.
2. Routine servicing maintains performance: **Rs 1,000 expense** for the period.
3. The clerk’s wrong entry still has equal debit and credit amounts. The trial balance can balance despite the classification error (an error of principle).
4. Correct the misclassified Rs 40,000: **Dr Equipment 40,000 / Cr Repairs expense 40,000**. Assume delivery and installation were already classified correctly.

**Check:** before depreciation, the error understated equipment and profit by Rs 40,000. Trial balance agreement is a useful arithmetic check, not proof that every account is correct.

</details>

**Rules you need**

| Rule or word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Debit (Dr) / credit (Cr) | The left / right side of an account. Total debits equal total credits for each transaction. |
| Asset | Something the business owns, including money customers owe. Increase: Dr; decrease: Cr. |
| Liability | Money the business owes. Increase: Cr; decrease: Dr. |
| Expense / drawings | A running cost / value the owner takes for private use. An increase is Dr. |
| Income / capital | Earnings / the owner's investment. An increase is Cr. |
| Debtor / creditor | A customer who owes us / a supplier we owe. Paying or receiving clears their balance; it is not another purchase or sale. |
| Capital expenditure | The cost of acquiring or improving a long-term asset, including getting it ready for use. Add it to the asset cost. |
| Revenue expenditure | A cost of running the business or maintaining existing performance. Charge it as an expense; goods for resale go through Purchases. |
| Trade / cash discount | Deduct trade discount before recording an invoice. Record cash discount separately when a debt is settled. |
| Ledger | One account for each asset, liability, expense, income or capital item. Post the transaction to each affected account. |
| Balance c/d / b/d | Carried down / brought down. Put the difference on the smaller side to balance, then bring it down on the opposite side next month. |
| Trial balance | A list of closing ledger balances. Assets, expenses and drawings normally have Dr balances; liabilities, income and capital have Cr balances. Equal totals alone do not prove everything is correct. |

**Step 1 — Ask what the expenditure achieves.** Capital expenditure buys or improves a long-term asset. Revenue expenditure runs the business or restores its existing performance. “Revenue expenditure” does not mean income received. If an invoice mixes improvement and repair, split it.

**Step 2 — Classify each item and explain why.**

| Item | Treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| i | Capital | Installation is necessary to bring the new machine into working condition; add it to the asset cost. |
| ii | Revenue | Hiring pays for use during the hire period; the business does not acquire the plant. Treat this as ordinary hire, not a purchase or finance arrangement. |
| iii | Capital | The cutlery equips a new dining room and will be used repeatedly. Treat it as the initial long-term equipment rather than a small routine replacement. |
| iv | Revenue | For this dealer, the vehicle is inventory: goods bought to sell to a customer. Record Purchases, not a long-term motor-vehicle asset. Inventory remains an asset until sold, but this question’s capital/revenue classification treats trading purchases as revenue items. |
| v | Capital | Patent rights give a long-term legal right. This is an intangible asset, meaning an asset without physical form. |
| vi | Revenue | Routine repair restores the machine’s existing condition. |
| vii | Revenue | Assume the reconditioned engine restores the existing van’s performance. It would be capital only if it improves capacity or extends useful life. |
| viii | Capital | The fees are directly connected to constructing the extension, so include them in that long-term asset’s cost. |

**Step 3 — Check the assumptions.** Treat the cutlery as the initial durable equipment of the new dining room: twelve dozen = 12 × 12 = **144 sets**. A dealer’s resale vehicle is trading inventory, not a fixed asset used in the business. The reconditioned engine is revenue only on the restoration assumption; capitalise it if it improves performance or extends the van’s life.

**Final check:** capital i, iii, v, viii = **4 items**; revenue ii, iv, vi, vii = **4 items**; 4 + 4 = **8 items**.
