## What is a budget?

:::key Definitions
- **Budget:** a detailed plan, **expressed in quantitative terms**, that specifies how an organisation will **acquire and use resources** during a **particular period**. In the lecture's words, "a **quantifiable action plan for a specified time period**" [[01:19]].
- **Budgetary control:** a system of **planning and controlling cost**.
:::

- Budgets flow from **strategic planning**. Every enterprise has goals: **profitability, growth, public service**.
- Strategy aims at **achieving and maintaining a competitive advantage** through **critical success factors**: the key strengths most responsible for the organisation's success.
- A budget is built on **past activities**: last year's figures plus planned changes [[02:29]].

### Purposes & functions of budgeting [[03:51]]

| Purpose | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Planning** | Plan annual operations: what and how much to produce and sell |
| **Facilitating communication & coordination** | Departments share information, giving **harmony between departments** |
| **Allocating resources** | Scarce resources go where they're needed |
| **Managing financial & operating performance** (control) | Compare progress against the budget; take corrective action |
| **Evaluating performance & providing incentives** | **Motivates managers** towards organisational goals |

### Steps in budgetary control

:::flow
1. Establish budgets
2. Measure actual performance
3. Compare actual with budget
4. Analyse causes of variances & report
:::

## Types of budgets

| Budget | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Master budget** | A **comprehensive set of budgets** covering **all phases** of operations for a period |
| **Operational budgets** | How operations will meet demand, using **material, labour and overhead** |
| **Financial budget** | How the organisation will **acquire its financial resources** |
| **Capital budget** | Plan for **acquiring (and disposing of) capital assets** |
| **Cash budget** | Expected **cash receipts and disbursements** |

The lecture groups them as **functional budgets → master budget → cash budget** [[07:36]].

**Master budget components:**

- **Operational:**
  - sales budget
  - production budget
  - direct material budget
  - direct labour budget
  - overhead budget
  - budgeted cost of goods manufactured and sold
  - selling, general and admin budget (R&D, marketing, customer service)
- **Financial:**
  - capital budget
  - cash budget
  - **budgeted income statement**
  - **budgeted statement of financial position**
  - budgeted statement of cash flows

:::flow
Sales budget
Production budget
Material usage → purchases
Labour & overhead budgets
Cash budget
Budgeted income statement & balance sheet
:::

### Sales forecasting: the starting point
The sales budget drives everything else. The factors are:

1. **past sales levels and trends**
2. **general economic trends**
3. **economic trends in the industry**
4. **other factors** expected to affect industry sales
5. **political and legal events**
6. **intended pricing policy**
7. **planned advertising and promotion**
8. **expected actions of competitors**
9. **new products** planned by the company or others
10. **market research studies**

## The functional budgets: formats

:::formula
**Sales budget:** Sales units × Selling price = Sales revenue
**Production (units) = Budgeted sales + Closing finished goods − Opening finished goods**
**Material usage = Production units × Material per unit**
**Material purchases = Usage + Closing raw materials − Opening raw materials**   (× cost per unit)
**Labour budget = Production units × Hours per unit × Rate per hour**
:::

**Why subtract opening and add closing?** **Opening** inventory is **already made**, so we don't produce it again. **Closing** inventory must **also be made** this period [[13:53]].

1. The question says overheads of **Rs 50m**, but the solution uses **Rs 55m** (that's what gives 214.5). With 50m, payments = 209.5, surplus = 170.5, and closing = **173.5**.
2. The video says the closing balance is **186.5m**. That's an arithmetic slip: 165.5 + 3 = **168.5m**.

Learn the method; check the numbers yourself.
:::

## The cash budget: rules

:::formula
**Closing cash = Opening cash + Cash inflows − Cash outflows**
:::

- It's prepared **weekly or monthly** so management can deal with **shortages** (arrange overdrafts or loans) or **surpluses** (invest them).
- **Only actual cash receipts and payments** are included.
- **Depreciation is always excluded**: it's not a cash flow.
- **Credit sales and purchases still outstanding at period end are excluded.** Include cash when it's **received or paid**, e.g. a 30-day credit sale in March is received in April.
- **The closing balance of period 1 = the opening balance of period 2.**

## Limitations of budgeting [[36:08]]

- **Based on estimates and assumptions**, so they **lack accuracy**.
- **Preparation takes time, cost and management effort.**
- **Lack of flexibility:** once fixed, budgets aren't changed for unexpected events (e.g. a **COVID lockdown**). This can **demotivate employees** who see the targets as unachievable [[37:33]].
- Budgets can encourage **budgetary slack** and "spend it or lose it" behaviour.

### Practice 14.1 — Sales to production to purchases

**Question:** Planned sales are 1,000 units at Rs 50. Opening finished goods are 100 units and desired closing finished goods are 150. Each unit produced uses 2 kg of material. Opening material is 200 kg and desired closing material is 300 kg. Material costs Rs 10/kg. There is no waste. Prepare sales, production and material purchase budgets.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 14.1</summary>

1. Sales revenue = 1,000 × 50 = **Rs 50,000**.
2. Production = sales + desired closing finished goods − opening finished goods = 1,000 + 150 − 100 = **1,050 units**.
3. Material needed for production = 1,050 × 2 = **2,100 kg**.
4. Material purchases = usage + desired closing material − opening material = 2,100 + 300 − 200 = **2,200 kg**.
5. Purchase budget value = 2,200 × 10 = **Rs 22,000**. This is purchase cost, not necessarily cash paid this month.

**Check:** finished goods 100 + production 1,050 − sales 1,000 = 150. Material 200 + purchases 2,200 − usage 2,100 = 300.

</details>

### Practice 14.2 — Prepare a cash budget with a collection lag

**Question:** Opening January cash is Rs 5,000. December credit sales were Rs 20,000; January sales are Rs 30,000 and February sales Rs 40,000. All sales are collected in the following month. Cash payments are January Rs 18,000 and February Rs 25,000, including all cash outflows. No other cash flows or financing apply.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 14.2</summary>

1. January receipts come from **December sales**, so receipts = **20,000**. January sales are not yet collected.
2. January closing cash = opening 5,000 + receipts 20,000 − payments 18,000 = **Rs 7,000**.
3. February opening cash = January closing cash = **7,000**.
4. February receipts come from **January sales**, so receipts = **30,000**.
5. February closing cash = 7,000 + 30,000 − 25,000 = **Rs 12,000**.

| Cash budget (Rs) | January | February |
|---|---:|---:|
| Opening cash | 5,000 | 7,000 |
| Receipts | 20,000 | 30,000 |
| Payments | (18,000) | (25,000) |
| **Closing cash** | **7,000** | **12,000** |

**Check:** February sales 40,000 are collected in March. Depreciation would be excluded because it is not a cash payment; asset purchases would be included when paid.

</details>
