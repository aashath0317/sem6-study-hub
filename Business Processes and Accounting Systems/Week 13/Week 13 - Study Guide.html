1What is project appraisal?

Definition

Project appraisal is the process of assessing and questioning proposals before resources are committed. It evaluates whether an investment project is worthwhile.

Key issues of project appraisal:

  • environmental factors
  • options (alternatives)
  • inputs
  • value for money
  • implementation
  • risk and uncertainty
Non-discounting methods (ignore the time value of money) Discounting methods (consider the time value of money)
Payback period Net present value (NPV)
Accounting rate of return (ARR) Internal rate of return (IRR)

2Non-discounting methods

Payback period

Definition

Payback is the length of time taken to repay the initial capital cost from the project's cash inflows 06:27.

  • Equal cash flows: payback = initial investment ÷ annual cash inflow.
  • Unequal cash flows: accumulate the cash flows year by year until the cost is recovered. Then add the fraction of the year: (amount still needed ÷ that year's cash flow).
  • Decision: accept if payback ≤ the benchmark period. Among mutually exclusive projects, choose the shortest payback.

Accounting rate of return (ARR)

ARR = Average annual profit ÷ Investment × 100%
PDF: ÷ initial cost. Video: ÷ average investment = (cost + scrap) ÷ 2
  • ARR compares accounting profit (after depreciation and tax), not cash flows, with the investment 27:43.
  • Decision: accept if ARR ≥ the predetermined minimum ARR (benchmark); choose the highest ARR 30:06.
$400,000 machine, 4 years, no scrap, benchmark 20% 32:28
Year 1 2 3 4 Total
Profit after 15% tax 51,000 68,000 59,500 76,500 255,000
  • Average profit = 255,000 ÷ 4 = 63,750
  • Average investment = (400,000 + 0) ÷ 2 = 200,000
  • ARR = 63,750 ÷ 200,000 = 31.875%. That's above 20%, so invest 35:41.

On initial cost (the PDF formula), ARR = 63,750 ÷ 400,000 = 15.9%. State which base you use.

Advantages Disadvantages
Easy to compute and understand Ignores the time value of money
Uses averages, so projects with different lives can be compared 36:31 Ignores cash flows: profit can be manipulated 37:14

3Time value of money

Definition

Money available today is worth more than the same amount in the future, because of its earning potential: money can earn interest and grow.

Three reasons 04:48:

  1. Opportunity cost: money in hand could earn a return elsewhere, such as a fixed deposit. Waiting means giving up the best alternative 05:29.
  2. Inflation: prices rise, so the purchasing power of money falls. Rs 1,000 buys fewer loaves next year 07:48.
  3. Uncertainty and risk: the future is uncertain (nobody predicted COVID in 2019), so investors demand a risk premium 09:47.
Present value: PV = FV ÷ (1 + i)ⁿ i = interest (discount) rate, n = number of years
Discount factor = 1 ÷ (1 + i)ⁿ
Equal in today's money 13:37

At 10%: receive 3,000 today, or receive 1,100 (year 1) + 1,210 (year 2) + 1,331 (year 3)?

Year Cash ÷ (1.1)ⁿ PV
1 1,100 1.100 1,000
2 1,210 1.210 1,000
3 1,331 1.331 1,000
Total 3,000

The two options have the same present value. Likewise, $110 in year 1 and $121 in year 2 are both worth $100 today 02:25.

4Net present value (NPV)

Steps 16:25:

  1. Identify the discount rate (cost of capital).
  2. Identify the project's cash flows.
  3. Discount each cash flow to present value.
  4. Add them and deduct the initial investment.
NPV = Σ CFₜ ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ − Initial investment
NPV Decision 17:45
> 0 Accept: the return beats an equal-risk alternative
< 0 Reject
= 0 Indifferent: accept if the project is necessary
Several projects with limited funds Choose the highest NPV

Advantages of NPV 24:56:

  • it uses the time value of money
  • it considers all cash flows over the project's life
  • it uses cash flows, not accounting profit (which can be manipulated)
  • it's consistent with maximising shareholder wealth

5Internal rate of return (IRR)

Definition

IRR is the discount rate that makes NPV = 0: discounted inflows exactly equal discounted outflows 26:22.

Accept if IRR ≥ the cost of capital (benchmark); reject if it's lower.

  • IRR helps measure the worth of an investment against internal standards of return.
  • It allows comparison of projects with different initial outlays.

Calculating IRR (trial and interpolation) 27:44:

  1. Find the NPV at a rate that gives a positive NPV (P).
  2. Find it at a higher rate that gives a negative NPV (N).
  3. Interpolate:
IRR = L + [P ÷ (P − N)] × (H − L)
L = lower rate, H = higher rate, P = NPV at L (positive), N = NPV at H (negative)
IRR example 30:26

Cost $400,000; inflows $200,000 a year for 3 years; benchmark 15%.

Rate Annuity factor (3 years) PV of inflows NPV
22% 2.0422 408,440 +8.4k (P)
24% 1.9813 396,260 −3.8k (N)

IRR = 22 + [8.4 ÷ (8.4 − (−3.8))] × (24 − 22) = 22 + (8.4 ÷ 12.2) × 2 = 23.38% 32:24

Since 23.38% > 15%, accept.

Watch the signs: P − N = 8.4 + 3.8.

IRR disadvantages:

  • complex
  • time-consuming
  • needs expertise to calculate and interpret 32:45
