Q1 II and III: Comment questions (3 marks each)

II. "The cost of an asset is depreciated over its useful life."

Partly true. Under LKAS 16, it's the depreciable amount (cost less residual value) that's spread over the useful life, on a systematic basis that reflects how the benefits are used. Land is usually not depreciated, because it has an unlimited life. Useful life and residual value are reviewed at least once a year.

III. "ROCE is more informative than the net profit ratio."

Agree. ROCE = profit before interest and tax / capital employed, so it shows how efficiently all the long-term funds are used, and you can compare it with the cost of borrowing or other investments. The net profit ratio only relates profit to sales. It ignores how much capital was needed to earn that profit, so two firms with the same margin can have very different ROCE.

II. "A master budget starts from a production budget." Comment

Disagree. The master budget normally starts from the sales budget, because sales are usually the principal budget factor (the limiting factor). Production is then planned as sales + closing stock − opening stock. Only if production capacity (or materials or labour) is the limiting factor would the production budget come first.

II and III. Accounting software; AIS vs ERP
  • Software: QuickBooks, Sage, Xero, Tally, SAP Business One, Microsoft Dynamics.
  • "AIS is broader than ERP": disagree. An ERP (e.g. SAP, Oracle) integrates all business processes (finance, HR, production, sales, supply chain) in one database. The AIS is the accounting part of it. So ERP is the broader system, and AIS is a subset (a module) of it.

7August 2020 — what's different

Q1: Same trial balance as 2023, plus one extra adjustment

ABC Enterprises (year to 31 March 2020) uses exactly the same figures as Mihinadee above. The one extra point:

7. A cash count at night showed Rs. 3,000 against a book balance of Rs. 5,000. Cash drawings weren't recorded.

So there's a further drawings of 2,000, and cash falls to 3,000:

  • Net profit: unchanged at 550,300, because drawings aren't an expense
  • Drawings = 4,000 (goods) + 2,000 (cash) = 6,000
  • Capital = 2,686,400 + 550,300 − 6,000 = 3,230,700
  • Total assets = 3,887,700

(c) AIS components (5 marks):

  1. People who use the system
  2. Procedures and instructions (manual and automated)
  3. Data about the organisation and its business activities
  4. Software used to process the data
  5. IT infrastructure (computers, peripherals, networks)
  6. Internal controls and security

(d) "Manual is more effective than computerised": disagree.

Computerised systems are faster, more accurate (automatic posting and totals), give real-time reports, store data more easily, and offer backup and audit trails. Manual systems are cheaper to start and don't depend on electricity or IT skills. Overall, computerised systems are more effective for most businesses, provided there are proper controls.

Q4: NPV, payback and ARR

i. ABC Ltd:

  • NPV = −8,320,000 + 3,411,000 × 0.847 + 4,070,000 × 0.718 + 5,824,000 × 0.609 + (2,065,000 + 900,000) × 0.516 = +Rs. 2,568,130, so invest
  • Payback: 3,411,000 + 4,070,000 = 7,481,000 after 2 years; 839,000 / 5,824,000 of year 3 more → 2.14 years

ii. ARR:

  • Average annual profit = 62,500 / 5 = 12,500
  • ARR on the initial investment = 12,500 / 45,000 = 27.8%
  • ARR on the average investment = 12,500 / 22,500 = 55.6%

iii. "Non-discounting methods are better": disagree.

  • Payback and ARR are simple and good for checking liquidity and risk.
  • But they ignore the time value of money, and payback ignores the cash flows after the payback period.
  • Discounting methods (NPV, IRR) use all the cash flows and their timing, so they're more appropriate for decisions. Non-discounting methods are useful as supporting measures.

10November 2019 — Section B

Q3: NPV and payback (Kapila PLC)
  • NPV = −7,320,000 + 3,420,000 × 0.847 + 4,090,000 × 0.718 + 5,924,000 × 0.609 + 2,965,000 × 0.516 = +Rs. 3,651,020. It's positive, so accept.
  • Payback = 1 + (7,320,000 − 3,420,000) / 4,090,000 = 1.95 years
  • Reliability of AIS: decisions, tax and audits depend on accurate, complete, timely data. Reliability comes from internal controls (authorisation, segregation of duties, access controls, backups, audit trails). An unreliable AIS leads to fraud, errors and bad decisions.
  • Pricing by buyer's location (adjusting the list price for shipping costs): geographical pricing, e.g. FOB-origin, uniform-delivered or zone pricing.

Time-rate system:

  • Advantages: simple; earnings are steady; quality doesn't suffer from rushing.
  • Disadvantages: no incentive for efficiency; needs supervision; slow and fast workers are paid the same.

Ratio analysis:

  • Benefits: compare performance over time and with competitors; spot trends; support decisions.
  • Limitations: based on historical data; different accounting policies distort comparisons; inflation; window dressing; ignores non-financial factors.
Q7: LKAS 16 plant cost (Company X, Rs. '000)
  • Capitalise: invoice 3,500 + freight 70 + customs and non-claimable VAT (400 − 250) 150 + agent's fee 35 + installation 185 + engineer's salary for the installation month 45 = 3,985
  • Expense: staff training 20
  • Receivable: claimable VAT 250

ii. Payroll software 900,000, used for 5 years: it meets the definition of an asset, because it's a resource controlled from a past event and is expected to give future economic benefits. Recognise it as an intangible asset and amortise it at 180,000 a year.

iii. Definitions:

  • Useful life: the period over which an asset is expected to be available for use (or the number of units it's expected to produce).
  • Scrap (residual) value: the estimated amount the entity would get from disposing of the asset now, less disposal costs, if it were already at the age and condition expected at the end of its life.

iv. Stock terms:

  • Re-order level: the stock level at which a new order is placed, e.g. max usage × max lead time.
  • EOQ: the order size that minimises total ordering and holding costs: √(2DC₀/C_h).

Production-related budgets (asked in 2025)
  • Production budget = budgeted sales + closing finished goods − opening finished goods
  • Material usage budget = production × material per unit
  • Material purchases budget = usage + closing material − opening material
  • Material cost = purchases (or usage) × price per unit
  • Labour budget = production × hours per unit × rate per hour
