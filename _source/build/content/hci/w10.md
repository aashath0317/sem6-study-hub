## Processor- vs predictor-oriented models

Processor models like GOMS look at **observable** input and output. Real reasoning is **implicit, nuanced and full of cognitive load**.

| | ⚙️ **Processor-oriented** | 🧠 **Predictor-oriented** |
|---|---|---|
| **Roots** | **Behaviourism** | **Cognitivism** |
| **Looks at** | **Externally observable actions**: keystrokes, mouse moves | **Unobservable internal reasoning**: memory recall, selective attention, decision branching, mental fatigue |
| **Human seen as** | A mechanical calculation unit | A thinking, feeling reasoner |
| **Example method** | GOMS, KLM (Week 9) | **Cognitive Task Analysis** (this week) |

## Cognitive Task Analysis (CTA) — 5 steps

**CTA** systematically **extracts, structures and validates the invisible cognitive processes** needed to complete complex tasks.

:::flow
1. Collect preliminary knowledge
2. Identify knowledge representations
3. Populate the representations
4. Analyse & verify
5. Format for design
:::

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **1. Collect preliminary knowledge** | **Observe real users in their natural context** to become familiar with the domain. You don't need to be an expert in it |
| **2. Identify knowledge representations** | Decide what **form** the knowledge takes: a **strict linear sequence**, an **unordered set of tasks**, or a **web of interconnected concepts** |
| **3. Populate the representations** | Gather what users actually **think, know and feel** using **think-aloud protocols**, including how they **recover from interruptions** and which **environmental cues** they use |
| **4. Analyse and verify** | **Review the findings with real users** to confirm the model matches their **actual workflow** |
| **5. Format results for design** | Turn the validated flows into **UI structures, blueprints and hierarchy trees** |

## Case study: the customer feedback thought stream

**On the surface**, giving feedback looks like three simple steps:

:::flow
Choose feedback method
Write / mention feedback
Submit feedback
:::

**In reality**, "write feedback" hides a **layered cognitive cycle**:

<figure><img src="Figure%2010.2.PNG" alt="Cognitive stream behind writing feedback"><figcaption>Figure 10.2: The actual cognitive stream inside "Write/mention feedback"</figcaption></figure>

1. **Recall the service experience** (memory)
2. **Assess the service quality** (reasoning and emotion)
3. **Mentally agree on and organise what to mention** (decision)

The user may loop through these several times. A processor model would see only "typing". CTA reveals the thinking that a good design should support, for example with prompts like *"How was the delivery speed?"*

## Hierarchical Task Analysis (HTA)

<figure><img src="Figure%2010.1.PNG" alt="Top level of the feedback task"><figcaption>Figure 10.1: The top level of "Provide feedback": choose method → write/mention → submit</figcaption></figure>


**HTA breaks high-level goals into nested sub-tasks, basic operations and plans.** It can be written as **text** or drawn as a **tree diagram**. Usually you do the **textual version first** [[v2 00:00]].

### Textual HTA

```
0. Provide feedback
   1. Choose feedback method
   2. Write / mention feedback
      2.1 Open email          ┐ email branch
      2.2 Write email         ┘
      2.3 Open form           ┐ form branch
      2.4 Fill form           ┘
   3. Submit feedback
      3.1 Send email (click Send)
      3.2 Submit form (click Submit)

Plan 0: do 1 – 2 – 3 in order
Plan 2: IF method = email THEN do 2.1, 2.2 in order ELSE do 2.3, 2.4 in order
Plan 3: IF method = email THEN do 3.1 ELSE do 3.2
```

:::key Plans
A **plan** says **in what order, and under what condition**, the sub-tasks are done. At any time **either** 2.1 + 2.2 **or** 2.3 + 2.4 is done, never both [[v2 02:35]].
:::


<figure><img src="Figure%2010.4.PNG" alt="HTA diagram view 1"><figcaption>Figure 10.4: The HTA diagram from the lesson, with plans: if email do 2.1, 2.2, else 2.3, 2.4; if email do 3.1, else 3.2</figcaption></figure>

### Reading the diagram: top to bottom, left to right [[v2 03:51]]
Start at the root and go **down**. At each node, **finish all its sub-branches before moving right**. Where a plan has a choice, follow **only the chosen branch**, then come back up and continue right.

### An alternative decomposition (View 2)

<figure><img src="Figure%2010.6.PNG" alt="HTA diagram view 2"><figcaption>Figure 10.6: The alternative decomposition: submit feedback holds all six sub-tasks, with the plan choosing email (2.1 to 2.3) or form (2.4 to 2.6)</figcaption></figure>


The same goal can be broken down differently. The PDF's second view puts **writing inside submitting**:

```
0. Provide feedback
   1. Choose feedback method
   2. Submit feedback
      2.1 Open email   2.2 Write email   2.3 Send email
      2.4 Open form    2.5 Fill form     2.6 Submit form
Plan 2: IF email do 2.1–2.3 ELSE do 2.4–2.6
```

:::tip There's no single "correct" HTA
Different analysts produce different but valid hierarchies. What matters is that the decomposition is **complete**, the **plans are clear**, and it matches **how users really work**.
:::

## Modularity & divide and conquer

Breaking tasks into structured sub-tasks reveals **universal modular patterns** that **transfer across applications**:

:::grid
**🛒 E-commerce checkout**

Every online store uses the physical metaphor of a **shopping cart** and **checkout counter**. Because this sub-task is **standardised**, vendors can **embed existing payment widgets** (PayPal, Stripe) instead of reinventing them.
+++
**🧩 Divide & conquer in UI design**

Hierarchy enables **abstraction**. Designers can isolate **solved sub-problems** (login, search, checkout), reuse them, and spend their creativity on **what makes their product unique**.
:::

:::exam
Typical questions:

- "Differentiate processor- and predictor-oriented models"
- "Explain the 5 steps of CTA"
- "Draw a textual **and** diagrammatic HTA (with plans) for task X", e.g. withdrawing cash from an ATM or booking a ticket

Always number the nodes (1, 1.1, 1.2…), write the **plans**, and remember **top to bottom, left to right**.
:::

## Quick revision sheet

| Concept | Remember it as |
|---|---|
| Processor-oriented | Behaviourism; observable actions (GOMS) |
| Predictor-oriented | Cognitivism; hidden reasoning, memory, attention |
| CTA | Uncovers invisible cognition |
| 5 steps | Collect → Identify representations → Populate (think-aloud) → Verify with users → Format for design |
| Feedback stream | Recall experience → Assess quality → Organise what to say |
| HTA | Goal → sub-tasks → operations + plans; textual first, then diagram |
| Plan | Order and conditions (IF email THEN 2.1, 2.2 ELSE 2.3, 2.4) |
| Traversal | Top to bottom, left to right; finish sub-branches first |
| Modularity | Standard sub-tasks (checkout, login) can be reused (PayPal, Stripe) |
