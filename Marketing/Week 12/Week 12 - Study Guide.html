1Before promoting

  1. Identify your unique features and competitive edge: your unique value proposition.
  2. Find the best ways to reach the target market: their internet literacy, time online, preference for print, phone and so on.
  3. Design a multi-stage promotional strategy. Each tool serves a different goal: personal selling builds close relationships, advertising reaches large audiences.

2Integrated marketing communication (IMC)

IMC

Integrating all promotional tools so they work together in coordination, not in isolation. All communications and messages are carefully connected.

  • Levels of integration: vertical, horizontal, internal, external.
  • A combined message has more impact than many separate ones.
  • A consistent, crystal-clear message can cut through the 500+ commercial messages consumers see each day.
  • IMC consolidates the image, creates dialogue, strengthens relationships and raises profit through effectiveness.

The new marketing communications

Three changes drove the new (integrated) model:

Change Meaning
Changing consumers Better informed and more involved. With the internet they create their own messages about brands, positive or negative (the Old Spice example)
Changing marketing strategies Away from mass marketing toward targeted segments and direct relationships
Advances in communication technology Social media (blogging, tweeting, status updates) builds stronger connections

3Promotion objectives

Objective Explanation
a) Stimulate demand The main objective: turn potential buyers into real buyers by highlighting benefits
b) Inform consumers Price, features, performance, quality, availability, changes, new products, special offers
c) Persuade consumers Show the product's superiority over competitors' products
d) Promote a new product Introduce it successfully against existing products, at every stage of adoption
e) Face competition Compete effectively, deter entry, push rivals out
f) Create or improve image Build brand and corporate image (Unilever, Sony, Philips, Hero Honda, Singer)

4The promotion mix

The balance of promotional tools used to create, maintain and grow demand. To balance them, ask: what's the most effective way to inform customers? Which methods? Aimed at whom? What's the budget and how is it allocated?

Tool Meaning Example
Advertising A paid, non-personal promotion by an identified sponsor. Builds a pull strategy and encourages a first trial TV, print, digital ads
Personal selling Face-to-face communication between a company representative and the consumer, to influence purchase Insurance agents, B2B sales reps
Sales promotion Short-term incentives to boost sales for a period (festivals, season-end): offers, discounts, paybacks, freebies, coupons Avurudu sales, "buy 1 get 1"
Public relations (PR) Build a favourable image through relationships with the public; publicity brings news to the market Press releases, sponsorships
Direct marketing Reach consumers directly, without intermediaries: email, SMS, fax Shoppers Stop SMSes about year-end sales and gold-card benefits

5Advertising

Advertising is drawing public attention to a product through paid announcements by an identified sponsor.

Benefits:

  • very cost-efficient per person for large audiences
  • repeatable across media
  • adds value and enhances the firm's image

Four types of advertising

Type Purpose When Example
Informative Explain features and benefits with facts; build primary demand New product categories or new products Dialog eZ Cash
Persuasive Build selective demand and brand preference; user-oriented; may use celebrities and discounts Competitive growth stage Teleshop / TV-shop ads
Comparison Present your product as better than a competitor's (side by side, on value or cost) Competitive markets Panadol vs paracetamol, Hutch vs Airtel, insurance and utility comparisons
Reminder Keep top-of-mind awareness; reinforce brand value Well-known brands, maturity or late PLC Coke ads during cricket tournaments

Advertising strategy: four principles

  1. Qualities of the product: purpose, features, advantages over alternatives. These define the message.
  2. State of the market: openness to purchase, competitors' share, the economy (luxury is hard to sell in a recession).
  3. Advertising goals: specific goals that give a roadmap and a way to measure effectiveness.
  4. Methodology: tone, qualities emphasised, medium (TV, magazines), ad location, and budget.

The plan's elements are the product and its advantages, the customer and their characteristics, alternative ways to inform buyers, and optimising choices within the budget. No plan is perfect: resources are always limited.

Two major elements:

  1. Creating the advertising message: something special and unique about the product.
  2. Selecting the media: cost-effectiveness. Many ad failures come from choosing the wrong media.

6Message strategy

Factors to consider:

  • target audience characteristics (age, location, attitudes)
  • media type
  • product factors (new vs established)
  • overall advertising objective (immediate sales vs initial awareness)

Steps: identify consumer benefits → develop meaningful, believable, distinctive appeals → create a creative concept.

1. Design the message

Decide what to say (content) and how to say it (structure and format).

2. The appeal

Appeal How it works Example
Rational Evidence and reason; appeals to self-interest "Saves 30% fuel"
Emotional Stirs positive or negative feelings (love, pride, joy, humour, fear) that motivate purchase Family moments in a milk-powder ad
Moral Appeals to the sense of what is right and proper "Don't drink and drive", charity campaigns

3. Message structure: three issues

  1. Draw a conclusion for the audience, or leave it to them?
  2. Put the strongest arguments first or last?
  3. One-sided (strengths only) or two-sided (strengths and admitted shortcomings)?

4. Message format

Use novelty and contrast (eye-catching pictures and headlines), distinctive formats (size, position), colour, shape, movement.

Clear marketing messages:

Characteristic Example
Get to the point "Our software reduces your manufacturing time."
Take the customer's viewpoint "It takes just two minutes, tops, to learn our product."
Use familiar language "We help your sales team close deals faster."
Feel informal "Without surprises. Ever."
Say something original "You'll save so much you'll think you won the lottery."
Need no explanation "We win more eyes for your advertisements."
Make you smile "It's rocket science meets brain surgery, but easy to use."

7Public relations

  • PR manages perceptions of the business to attract new customers and strengthen existing loyalty.
  • It uses unpaid channels (media, internet, networks, community).
  • PR is more credible than paid advertising, because the result is shaped by a third party.
  • Marketing promotes products; PR promotes awareness and behaviour change.
PR tool What it is
Media relations Pitch interesting stories (facts, customer stories, spokesperson news) to newspapers, magazines, TV and the internet. Good contact lists also help manage crises
Advertorials Ads written like news stories: they borrow the paper's credibility (also on TV)
Social media Bypass the media and talk to customers directly; respond quickly to negative perceptions
Newsletters Print or email: regular contact, new launches, stronger relationships
Brochures and catalogues Keep customers thinking of you; drive traffic to the website or store
Business events Exposure, launches, answering doubts, market research, building mailing lists
Speaking engagements Talks that position the firm as a leader; networking
Sponsorships and partnerships Support non-profit causes for goodwill and loyalty; associate the brand with ethics
Employee relations Keep staff informed (email, newsletters, intranet)
Media tour A spokesperson, expert or celebrity tours target cities (e.g. book launches with readings and signings)
Financial relations Relationships with investors, analysts and financial journalists (earnings affect stock prices)

8Sales promotion

AMA definition

"Media and non-media marketing pressure applied for a predetermined, limited period to stimulate trial, increase consumer demand or improve product availability."

Used to launch new products, sell out inventory, attract customers and lift sales temporarily.

Objectives:

  • create a market for new products (penetration pricing, offers, discounts, scarcity)
  • remain competitive against rivals' short-term tactics
  • gain dealers' trust (more sales means more dealer income)
  • enter new markets
  • increase brand awareness
  • woo existing customers against competitors' poaching

Three sales-promotion strategies

  • Pull: customers "pull" the product from the company through communication, seasonal discounts and finance schemes.
  • Push: convince intermediaries to push the product through the channel to consumers, with trade promotions and personal selling aimed at resellers, dealers, distributors and agents.
  • Hybrid: both, e.g. coupons for consumers plus incentives for merchants.

Consumer vs trade promotions

Consumer sales promotion (to end users)

  • Free samples: awareness and a sense of ownership
  • Free gifts: more for less
  • Discounts and coupons
  • Exchange schemes: value for old products
  • Finance schemes: make expensive items affordable
  • Shipping schemes: remove freight friction
  • Bundle discounts: clear unsold stock
  • Bulk-purchase deals

Trade sales promotion (to dealers, distributors, agents)

  • Point-of-purchase (POP) displays: free display units
  • Trade shows
  • Push money (spiffs): e.g. a Rs 50 bonus per unit of A, Rs 30 per unit of B
  • Deal loaders: gifts for ordering a set quantity
  • Trade deals: concessions to promote a product for a limited time
  • Buying allowances: discounts for ordering a set number

Aims: more shelf space, motivated dealers, indirect sales growth.

9Push vs pull channel policies

🧪 Try it — Push vs pull — watch the flow
Push Pull
Main tools Sales force, trade promotion, personal selling Heavy advertising, consumer promotion
Direction Product pushed down the channel to the consumer Consumer demand pulls the product: consumer → retailer → wholesaler → manufacturer
Example Nokia via retailers; Mars via distributors "Intel Inside"
Best practice

A good promotional strategy combines push and pull.

10Socially responsible marketing communication

  • Social responsibility: businesses should be good citizens, combining profit-making with actions that support society (local, national, global). It attracts customers who want their purchases to make a meaningful impact.
  • Examples: recyclable packaging, ads raising awareness of community problems, giving part of the proceeds to charity.
Greenwashing backfires

Deceptively promoting environmentally friendly practices or products shows consumers the firm isn't really committed, and eventually harms the brand and profitability. Communicate your responsible behaviour strategically and honestly.
