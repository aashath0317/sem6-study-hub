Graphs are discrete structures made of vertices and edges that connect them. They model a huge range of problems:

  • Competition between species in an ecological niche
  • Who influences whom in an organisation
  • Round-robin tournament outcomes
  • Acquaintanceships between people and links between websites
  • Roadmaps and assigning jobs to employees
  • Whether a circuit fits on a planar circuit board
  • Telling apart chemical compounds with the same formula but different structures
  • Whether two computers are connected in a network

வரைபுகள், முனைகளாலும் அவற்றை இணைக்கும் விளிம்புகளாலும் ஆன தனித்த கட்டமைப்புகள். பலதரப்பட்ட பிரச்சினைகளை மாதிரியாக்குகின்றன:

  • நிறுவனத்தில் யார் யாரைச் செல்வாக்குப்படுத்துகின்றனர்

The Königsberg bridge problem
  • Königsberg (Prussia) lay on the Pregel river, which split the city into four landmasses (including the island of Kneiphopf).
  • Seven bridges linked them.
  • Residents asked: can you leave home, cross each bridge exactly once, and return home?
  • Leonhard Euler (1707–1783) solved it. He turned each landmass into a vertex (A, B, C, D) and each bridge into an edge. His method is considered the birth of graph theory.
Euler's answer: no

For a finite undirected graph whose non-isolated vertices are connected, a closed walk using every edge exactly once exists iff every vertex has even degree (you leave a landmass as often as you enter it).

In Königsberg the degrees are A = 5, B = 3, C = 3, D = 3, all odd, so it's impossible. Load the Königsberg preset in the graph lab below to see it.

3What is a graph?

Graph அடிப்படை

Graph என்பது vertices மற்றும் edges சேர்ந்த அமைப்பு. Degree ஒரு vertex-க்கு சேர்ந்த edges எண்ணிக்கை; undirected loop இரண்டு முறை எண்ணப்படும். ஒரு படம் வேறுபட்டுப் பார்த்தாலும் இணைப்பு ஒரே மாதிரி இருக்கலாம்.

மேலும் புரிந்துகொள்ள · தேர்வுக் குறிப்புகள்

Finite graph-க்கு vertices எண்ணிக்கை finite; loops ஒரு vertex-ஐ அதனுடனே இணைக்கும்; parallel edges அதே endpoint pair-ஐ மீண்டும் இணைக்கும். Undirected degree sum Σdeg(v)=2|E|. Directed graph-இல் indegree/outdegree தனியாக. Total indegree=total outdegree=|E|. ஒரு arrow-ஐ undirected edge போல degree கணக்கில் கலக்காதீர்கள்.

G=(V,E),edge endpoints;loop same endpoint;parallel edges same pair. Undirected loopdegree-க்கு2 contribution;Σdegree=2|E|;directed indegrees sum=outdegrees sum=|E|. Finite simple nvertices-க்கு maxdegree n−1. Loops/multiple allowed என்றால் அந்த bound பொதுவாக இல்லை.

Definition 1

A graph G = (V, E) consists of:

  • V: a non-empty set of vertices (nodes)
  • E: a set of edges

Each edge has one or two vertices associated with it, called its endpoints. An edge connects its endpoints.

Degree of a vertex and the handshaking theorem (examined: both 2023 papers)

Degree
  • The degree deg(v) of a vertex in an undirected graph is the number of edges at v. A loop counts twice.
  • deg(v) = 0: an isolated vertex. deg(v) = 1: a pendant vertex.
  • In a simple graph with n vertices, every degree is at most n − 1 (v can join each other vertex once).
  • In a directed graph, deg⁻(v) is the in-degree (arrows in) and deg⁺(v) the out-degree (arrows out).
Handshaking theorem: Σ deg(v) = 2|E|
Each edge has two ends, so it adds 2 to the total degree.
Directed graphs: Σ deg⁻(v) = Σ deg⁺(v) = |E|
Corollary

An undirected graph has an even number of vertices of odd degree. (The total is even, so the odd degrees must pair up.)

Example: a graph has 10 edges. Its degrees add up to 2 × 10 = 20.

Can a graph with these degrees exist?

Check in this order and stop at the first failure:

  1. Sum must be even (handshaking). Any graph type.
  2. Simple graph: no degree above n − 1, and 0 and n − 1 can't both appear (a vertex joined to everyone means nobody is isolated).
  3. Simple graph, final test (Havel–Hakimi): sort the degrees largest first, remove the largest d, subtract 1 from the next d degrees, and repeat. If you ever get a negative number, or need more vertices than are left, it's impossible; if everything becomes 0, it's possible, and the steps tell you how to draw it.
  4. Multigraph (no loops): possible exactly when the sum is even and the largest degree ≤ the sum of all the others.
  5. Pseudograph (loops allowed): possible exactly when the sum is even.

If it is possible, draw it and label each vertex with its degree. If it isn't, name the rule it breaks with the numbers, e.g. "the degree sum is 19, which is odd, but Σ deg(v) = 2|E| must be even".

The pigeonhole principle and the "two equal degrees" proof

Pigeonhole principle

If k + 1 or more objects go into k boxes, at least one box holds two or more objects.

Proof (both 2023 papers): every simple graph with n ≥ 2 vertices has two vertices of equal degree
  1. Let G be a simple graph with n ≥ 2 vertices. Each degree is one of the n values 0, 1, …, n − 1.
  2. Case 1: some vertex has degree n − 1. It is joined to every other vertex, so no vertex has degree 0. The degrees lie in {1, …, n − 1}: n − 1 values.
  3. Case 2: no vertex has degree n − 1. The degrees lie in {0, …, n − 2}: again n − 1 values.
  4. In both cases, n vertices (pigeons) share at most n − 1 degree values (boxes).
  5. By the pigeonhole principle, two vertices have the same degree. ∎

Marks go to: the range of degrees, the reason 0 and n − 1 can't both appear, and naming the pigeonhole principle.

Directed graphs (digraphs)

Some links work in one direction only (simplex links), e.g. heavy traffic one way. So we use directed edges (arcs), drawn as arrows 01:42.

Definition 2 — directed graph

A directed graph (digraph) G = (V, E) has a non-empty vertex set V and a set E of directed edges. Each directed edge is associated with an ordered pair (u, v): it starts at u and ends at v.

Ordered vs unordered pairs 03:06
  • {u, v} (undirected): you can travel u → v or v → u.
  • (u, v) (directed): you must start at u and end at v. San Francisco → Denver does not allow Denver → San Francisco.

Graph types in one table (memorise):

Type Loops (edge from a vertex to itself)? Several edges between the same 2 vertices? Edge direction
Simple graph No No Undirected
Multigraph No Yes Undirected
Pseudograph Yes Yes Undirected
Directed graph Yes No (one per direction) Directed
Mixed graph Yes Yes Both directed and undirected

Q1. Which graph has each edge joining two different vertices and no two edges joining the same pair?

(a) simple (b) regular (c) pseudograph (d) directed

Answer: (a) simple graph

“Two different vertices” = no loops; “no two edges on the same pair” = no multiple edges → simple graph. (Regular = every vertex has the same degree — a different idea.)

Q2. A graph with both directed and undirected edges is a:

(a) directed multigraph (b) mixed graph (c) directed graph (d) simple directed graph

Answer: (b) mixed graph

Mixed = a mix of arrows and plain lines.

Q3. People who can influence the thinking of others are modelled by an ______.

(a) roadmap graph (b) acquaintanceship graph (c) web graph (d) influence graph

Answer: (d) influence graph

Influence graph: a directed edge a → b means “a influences b” (direction matters). Acquaintanceship graph: an undirected edge means two people know each other.

Q4. A highway system: cities are vertices, with an edge if there is an interstate highway between them. Which graph?

(a) multigraph (b) simple graph (c) directed graph (d) pseudograph

Answer: (b) simple graph

The question only asks whether a highway exists between two cities (yes/no), not how many, and roads go both ways → undirected, at most one edge per pair → simple graph. (If it counted each highway separately it would be a multigraph.)

Q5. A round-robin tournament can be modelled using a ______.

(a) directed graph (b) mixed graph (c) multigraph (d) simple graph

Answer marked by the quiz: (c) multigraph

The quiz accepted multigraph. Note: the Rosen textbook models a round-robin tournament as a directed graph (an edge a → b means team a beat team b). For this MCQ, pick the quiz's answer; in a written answer, explain the model you use.

Q6. Which property does a simple graph not need to have?

(a) no loops or multiple edges (b) must be unweighted (c) must be connected (d) all of the above

Answer: (c) must be connected

A simple graph only forbids loops and multiple edges. It can be in several separate pieces (disconnected) and still be simple.

Q7. Which is true?

(a) no edges and no vertices (b) no edges and many vertices (c) none (d) many edges and no vertices

Answer marked by the quiz: (d) — but be careful

The quiz key marks (d). Logically, though, an edge must join two vertices, so a graph cannot have edges without vertices. The statement that is really true is (b): a graph can have vertices and no edges (an empty or null graph). For the MCQ, remember the quiz marked (d); for a written answer, use the correct reasoning: every edge needs endpoints.

Q8. G has v vertices and e edges, is connected and has no cycles. Which is true?

(a) v = e (b) none (c) v + 1 = e (d) v = e + 1

Answer: (d) v = e + 1

“Connected with no cycles” is the definition of a tree. A tree with v vertices has exactly v − 1 edges, so e = v − 1 → v = e + 1. Check: 3 vertices in a line A–B–C have 2 edges ✓.

The statement that is really true is (b): a graph can have vertices and no edges (an empty or null graph). For the MCQ, remember the quiz marked (d); for a written answer, use the correct reasoning: every edge needs endpoints.

Q8. G has v vertices and e edges, is connected and has no cycles. Which is true?

(a) v = e (b) none (c) v + 1 = e (d) v = e + 1

Answer: (d) v = e + 1

“Connected with no cycles” is the definition of a tree. A tree with v vertices has exactly v − 1 edges, so e = v − 1 → v = e + 1. Check: 3 vertices in a line A–B–C have 2 edges ✓.
