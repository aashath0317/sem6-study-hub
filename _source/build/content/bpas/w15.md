## Standard cost & standard costing

:::key Definitions
- **Standard cost:** a **predetermined (planned) estimated unit cost** of a product, component or service, used for **stock valuation and control** [[04:21]]. It's what a unit **should** cost **under efficient operating conditions**.
- **Standard costing:** a **control technique** that **compares standard costs and revenues with actual results** to obtain **variances**, which are used to **stimulate improved performance** [[03:08]].
:::

:::example The carpenter [[02:16]]
A carpenter targets **Rs 15,000 per chair**, but the actual cost is **Rs 18,000**. That's a **Rs 3,000 adverse variance**. He investigates **why** and controls that cost next time. This is the core idea of standard costing.
:::

**Standard costing involves:**

:::flow
1. Set standards
2. Ascertain actual results
3. Compare → variances
4. Investigate & take action
:::

**Uses of standard costs:**

- **cost control**
- **performance evaluation**
- **stock valuation**
- **setting selling prices** [[05:31]]

### Standard vs budget

| | **Standard** | **Budget** |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | **Per unit** of product or activity | **Total** activity or operation |
| Example [[07:51]] | Rs 3 per unit | 10,000 units × 3 = **Rs 30,000** |

**Similarities:**

- both are **forecasts about the future**
- both are **control tools**

They're interrelated: **budget = standard × budgeted activity**.

## Setting standards

**Two approaches** [[08:51]]:

1. **Historical records:** use past experience of materials, labour and overhead per unit. It's simple.
2. **Engineering studies:** a technical study of the input–output relationship. It's more complex but more precise.

| Component | Quantity standard | Price or rate standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct material** | **Bill of materials (BOM)** per product, allowing for **unavoidable** waste (avoidable waste is not allowed) [[11:44]] | **Purchasing department**: contracts, supplier discussions, forecast price movements |
| **Direct labour** | **Standard hours** for an average worker to do the job | **Contractual wage rate** [[13:37]] |
| **Overheads** | Standard hours (absorption base) | **Standard overhead absorption rate**. Fixed overheads don't vary in the short run, but are absorbed per unit for **financial reporting and inventory valuation** [[14:55]] |

## Types of standards

| Type | Based on | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Ideal** | **Perfect conditions**: no waste, spoilage, inefficiency, idle time or breakdowns [[17:15]] | **Unattainable**, so it **demotivates** |
| **Attainable** | Allows for **normal waste and inefficiency**; realistic but **challenging** [[18:29]] | **Best for motivation** ✓ |
| **Current** | **Current** conditions, including current waste and inefficiency [[19:15]] | **Doesn't try to improve** efficiency |
| **Basic** | **Old standards, never changed** [[19:45]] | **Least useful**; mainly for showing long-term trends |

## Standard cost card

The **standard cost card** shows the **full details of the standard cost of each product**, one card per product. It's **confidential**: for **top management** decision-making only [[20:20]].

**A = adverse** (actual cost > standard, or actual revenue < standard): **reduces profit**.

## Variance formulas

:::formula
**DIRECT MATERIAL**
**Price variance** = (AQ × AP) − (AQ × SP) = Actual cost − Standard cost of actual quantity
**Usage variance** = (AQ − SQ) × SP   (SQ = standard quantity for the **actual output**)
:::

:::formula
**DIRECT LABOUR**
**Rate variance** = (AH × AR) − (AH × SR)
**Efficiency variance** = (AH − SH) × SR   (SH = standard hours for actual output)
:::

:::formula
**VARIABLE OVERHEAD**
**Expenditure variance** = Actual VOH − (AH × standard VOH rate)
**Efficiency variance** = (AH − SH) × standard VOH rate
:::

:::formula
**FIXED OVERHEAD**
**Total variance** = Actual FOH − **Absorbed** FOH (actual output × FOAR)
**Expenditure variance** = Actual FOH − **Budgeted** FOH
**Volume variance** = Absorbed − Budgeted = (Actual output − Budgeted output) × FOAR per unit
:::

:::formula
**SALES**
**Selling price variance** = (Actual price − Standard price) × Actual units sold
**Sales volume variance** = (Actual units − Budgeted units) × **standard profit per unit** (absorption costing) or × **standard contribution per unit** (marginal costing)
:::

:::tip Sign check
For **costs**, actual > standard is **adverse**. For **revenue and volume**, actual > standard is **favourable**. The two sub-variances always add up to the total.
:::

:::example Worked variance example (built on the product A card)
**Data:**

- **Budget:** 1,000 units; budgeted fixed overhead Rs 18,000 (FOAR Rs 18 per unit); standard price Rs 180 (standard profit 180 − 134 = Rs 46).
- **Actual:**
  - **produced and sold** 1,100 units at Rs 175
  - **materials** 2,300 kg costing Rs 24,150
  - **labour** 3,200 hours costing Rs 78,400
  - **overheads:** VOH Rs 26,000; FOH Rs 19,500

**Standards for the actual output:**

- **SQ** = 1,100 × 2 = **2,200 kg**
- **SH** = 1,100 × 3 = **3,300 hours**

| Variance | Working | Rs | |
|---|---|---:|---|
| DM price | 24,150 − 2,300 × 10 | 1,150 | **A** |
| DM usage | (2,300 − 2,200) × 10 | 1,000 | **A** |
| DL rate | 78,400 − 3,200 × 24 | 1,600 | **A** |
| DL efficiency | (3,200 − 3,300) × 24 | 2,400 | **F** |
| VOH expenditure | 26,000 − 3,200 × 8 | 400 | **A** |
| VOH efficiency | (3,200 − 3,300) × 8 | 800 | **F** |
| FOH expenditure | 19,500 − 18,000 | 1,500 | **A** |
| FOH volume | (1,100 − 1,000) × 18 | 1,800 | **F** |
| *FOH total (check)* | 19,500 − 1,100 × 18 = 19,500 − 19,800 | 300 | **F** ✓ |
| Selling price | (175 − 180) × 1,100 | 5,500 | **A** |
| Sales volume | (1,100 − 1,000) × 46 | 4,600 | **F** |

**Interpretation:**

- Workers were **efficient**: fewer hours than standard.
- But they were **paid a higher rate**, and **materials cost more and more were used**. Did cheaper, poor-quality material slow anything? Here it didn't, but **investigate the interdependence**.
- **Selling more units at a lower price** gave a **net Rs 900 adverse** sales effect.
:::

## Advantages & limitations

| **Advantages** [[22:24]] | **Limitations** [[25:17]] |
|---|---|
| **Predicts future costs** for decision-making | **Too much emphasis on direct labour**; modern production is capital-intensive |
| Gives employees a **target**, which **motivates** them | Needs **repetitive operations and homogeneous output**, so it's unsuited to **non-standardised or customised** production |
| **Evaluates managerial performance** | Hard to apply in **service organisations** |
| **Helps set budgets** (standard × activity) | Built for **stable environments**; standards **go out of date** as prices change |
| Acts as a **control device**: management by exception | Assumes **performance to standard is acceptable**, which conflicts with **continuous improvement** |
| **Simplifies** costing for **profit measurement and inventory valuation** | **Assigning responsibility** for variances is difficult (they're interdependent) |
| | **Reporting delay**: variances arrive late |
| | **Behavioural issues**: unattainable standards demotivate; blame culture |

<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Q3</summary>

**Step 1 — Keep the two drivers separate.** Plus uses labour hours at 10; Gold uses machine hours at 12. Do not combine raw hours into one unweighted number. Standard hours for actual output: Plus 4,500 × 2 = 9,000; Gold 5,200 × 1 = 5,200.

**Step 2 — Flex overhead to actual hours for expenditure variance.** Actual hours at standard rates = 10,000 × 10 + 5,000 × 12 = **160,000**. Expenditure variance = 160,000 − 175,000 = **15,000 A**.

**Step 3 — Value efficiency separately by driver.** Plus: (9,000 − 10,000) × 10 = 10,000 A. Gold: (5,200 − 5,000) × 12 = 2,400 F. Net = **7,600 A**. Actual expenditure is not split by product, so individual product expenditure variances cannot be calculated.

**Final check:** allowed standard cost 9,000 × 10 + 5,200 × 12 = 152,400; less actual 175,000 = **22,600 A**, equal to 15,000 A + 7,600 A.

</details>

## Beginner practice — questions and step-by-step answers

**Week 15 learning path:** Week 15: Standard cost card → Variance formulas.

**Start from zero:** A standard is the planned input or price for a unit of output. A variance is the difference between actual and standard. Favourable (F) cost variances mean lower costs; adverse (A) mean higher costs. Compare with the standard for actual output.

These are **new beginner learning questions**, not official past-paper questions or a replacement for the course-paper practice above. All amounts are in Sri Lankan rupees (Rs) unless stated otherwise.

**How to practise:** read the question, write your own attempt, then open the worked answer. Follow the reason for each step. Use the final check to find mistakes; mark this section understood only when you can explain the method.

### Practice 15.1 — Material price and usage variances

**Question:** Standard material is 2 kg per finished unit at Rs 10/kg. Actual output is 100 units. Actual material purchased and used is 220 kg at Rs 11/kg; there is no material stock change. Calculate price, usage and total material cost variances. Use actual minus standard: positive cost differences are adverse.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 15.1</summary>

1. Standard quantity for actual output (SQ) = 100 × 2 = **200 kg**. Standard price (SP) = **Rs 10/kg**.
2. Actual quantity (AQ) = **220 kg**; actual price (AP) = **Rs 11/kg**; actual cost = 220 × 11 = **Rs 2,420**.
3. Price variance = AQ × (AP − SP) = 220 × (11 − 10) = **Rs 220 A**. Each kg cost Rs 1 more than planned.
4. Usage variance = SP × (AQ − SQ) = 10 × (220 − 200) = **Rs 200 A**. Production used 20 kg more than allowed for 100 units.
5. Total cost variance = actual cost − standard cost of actual output = 2,420 − (200 × 10) = **Rs 420 A**.

**Check:** 220 A + 200 A = 420 A. A formula using standard minus actual reverses the sign; the A/F meaning must stay the same.

</details>

### Practice 15.2 — Labour rate and efficiency variances

**Question:** Standard labour is 3 hours per unit at Rs 20/hour. Actual output is 100 units. Actual hours are 280 and actual labour cost is Rs 6,160. Calculate rate, efficiency and total labour variances. Use actual minus standard for cost differences.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 15.2</summary>

1. Standard hours for actual output (SH) = 100 × 3 = **300 hours**. Standard rate (SR) = **Rs 20/hour**.
2. Actual rate = 6,160 ÷ 280 = **Rs 22/hour**.
3. Rate variance = actual cost − AH × SR = 6,160 − 280 × 20 = **Rs 560 A**. Labour cost more per hour.
4. Efficiency variance = (AH − SH) × SR = (280 − 300) × 20 = −400, or **Rs 400 F**. Fewer hours were used than standard.
5. Total variance = actual cost − SH × SR = 6,160 − 300 × 20 = **Rs 160 A**.

**Check:** 560 A less 400 F = 160 A. Higher efficiency can partly offset a higher hourly rate. A variance suggests something to investigate; it does not prove who caused it.

</details>
