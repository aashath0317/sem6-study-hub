Inventory Costing

Lecture on inventory and material costing.

1Inventory & LKAS 2

Definition

Inventory is the raw materials, work-in-progress and finished goods that are the part of a business's assets ready, or that will be ready, for sale.

Raw materialsWork in progress (WIP)Finished goods

LKAS 2 objective: prescribe the accounting for inventories:

  • determining cost
  • recognising the expense, including any write-down to NRV
  • the cost formulas used
The fundamental principle of LKAS 2

Inventories are stated at the LOWER of cost and net realisable value (NRV).

What goes into the cost of inventory

Include Exclude
Purchase costs: price + non-refundable taxes, transport and handling, net of trade discounts and subsidies Abnormal waste
Conversion costs: direct labour + fixed and variable production overheads Storage costs
Other costs to bring inventory to its present location and condition Administrative overheads not related to production
Selling costs
Foreign-exchange differences on recent foreign-currency purchases
Interest on deferred-settlement purchases

Net realisable value

NRV = Estimated selling price − Estimated costs of completion − Estimated costs to sell
  • Write-downs to NRV are an expense in the period they happen. Reversals are recognised in the income statement when they happen.
  • NRV can be compared item by item (the item method) or by groups of similar items (the batch method).
  • Cost formulas for interchangeable items: FIFO or weighted average (LIFO is not allowed).
  • Expense recognition: when inventory is sold, its carrying amount becomes cost of goods sold. Write-downs and losses are expensed when they occur.
Lower of cost and NRV

Item-by-item total 2,150

Comparing the totals instead (cost 2,300 vs NRV 2,450) would give 2,300. That hides B's loss, which is why the item method is preferred.

2Function 1: managing purchases

Centralised vs decentralised buying

Centralised buying Decentralised buying
Advantages Consistent buying policy · better control · expert buying staff · avoids excess stock Lower transport cost · rejections, shortages and excess receipts settled quickly · local purchase benefits (low or seasonal prices)
Disadvantages Less flexible · high initial cost · high transport cost High stock-holding cost

The purchase procedure

  1. Purchase requisition (when?)
  1. Determine quantity (how much?)
  1. Select supplier (where?)
  1. Purchase order & follow-up
  1. Receive & inspect (GRN)
  1. Check invoice & pass for payment
Step Details
1. Purchase requisition (PR) Request to buy, usually in triplicate: to purchasing, to production control, and kept by the initiating department
2. Quantity In line with production and sales plans. Optimum quantity, no overstocking (EOQ)
3. Supplier Inquiries and tenders or quotations → analyse tenders → select the best source
4. Purchase order A large firm may make 5 copies: supplier, receiving department, accounts, the requisitioning department, and one kept by purchasing
5. Receive and inspect Unload, unpack, mark; check quality and quantity; move into the factory. Prepare the goods received note (GRN), the basis of entries in the stores ledger
6. Invoice Check against the PO and GRN, then pass for payment

Material cost

Invoice price + freight + excise duty + non-returnable containers (as LKAS 2's purchase cost).

  • Cash discount is a financial item, not deducted from material cost.
  • Returnable containers: only the net cost (charge − credit on return) is included.
Lecture example: material cost of 6,000 units from Mr. Naleen
  • Quotation: 2,000 units @ Rs 5.00; 4,000 @ Rs 4.75; 6,000 @ Rs 4.00
  • Trade discount 25%; cash discount 5% within 10 days
  • Freight Rs 200 per order
  • Containers Rs 0.50 each, one per 100 units, credit Rs 0.30 each if returned within two months

3Function 2: storing materials & stock levels

Why efficient storing?

  • keep stores in proper custody
  • protect them from theft, deterioration, evaporation, pilferage
  • maintain a smooth, regular flow to production

Store locations: centralised, decentralised, or a combination.

Centralised stores: advantages Disadvantages
Better layout, less equipment, better supervision, economy in cost, better stock forecasting High transport cost, greater fire risk, delays to production

Stock control levels

Level Formula Meaning
Re-order level (ROL) Max consumption × max lead time (or min stock + avg consumption × avg lead time) When the storekeeper raises a purchase requisition
Maximum stock level ROL + ROQ − (min consumption × min lead time) Stock shouldn't rise above this (avoids blocking capital)
Minimum stock level (safety or buffer stock) ROL − (avg consumption × avg lead time) Stock shouldn't fall below this
Average stock level (Max + Min) ÷ 2, or Min + ½ ROQ
Lecture example: stock levels
  • ROQ = 36,000 units
  • Lead time 2–4 months (average 3)
  • Max consumption 9,000 per month; normal consumption 6,000 per month
  • Assume minimum consumption = 2 × 6,000 − 9,000 = 3,000, since normal is the average of max and min
Level Working Units
Re-order level 9,000 × 4 36,000
Minimum level 36,000 − (6,000 × 3) 18,000
Maximum level 36,000 + 36,000 − (3,000 × 2) 66,000
Average level (66,000 + 18,000) ÷ 2 42,000 (or 18,000 + ½ × 36,000 = 36,000)

Economic order quantity (EOQ)

EOQ = √( 2 × D × Co ÷ Ch )
D = annual demand · Co = cost of placing one order · Ch = holding cost per unit per year

EOQ assumptions:

  • perfectly forecastable, even demand
  • constant lead time
  • constant material price
  • constant holding cost per unit
Lecture example: EOQ
  • Monthly usage 250, so D = 3,000 a year
  • Co = Rs 30
  • Unit cost Rs 5, holding cost 10%, so Ch = Rs 0.50

EOQ = √(2 × 3,000 × 30 ÷ 0.5) = √360,000 = 600 units

4Function 3: pricing material issues — FIFO & weighted average

Method Idea Effect when prices rise
FIFO (first in, first out) Issues are priced at the oldest batches first Issues at older (lower) prices, so closing stock is at the latest (higher) prices and profit is higher
Weighted average cost (WAC) After each receipt, compute average = total value ÷ total units; issue at that average Smooths price changes
Lecture example: pillows, January 2019
  • 1 Jan: balance 200 @ Rs 500
  • 10 Jan: purchased 300 @ Rs 600
  • 15 Jan: issued 250
  • 20 Jan: purchased 100 @ Rs 650
  • 24 Jan: issued 175

FIFO
