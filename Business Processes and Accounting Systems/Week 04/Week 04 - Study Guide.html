Capital vs Revenue Expenditure, the Ledger & the Trial Balance

Telling capital expenditure from revenue expenditure, the general ledger and its classification, posting and balancing ledger accounts, preparing a trial balance, its purposes and its limitations (errors it can't detect), with a full worked example.

1Capital vs revenue expenditure

Spending on fixed assets is either capital or revenue expenditure. The distinction matters because only capital expenditure is included in the cost of the fixed asset. Revenue expenditure is an expense in the income statement.

Capital expenditure Revenue expenditure
Definition Costs of acquiring a fixed asset, plus later spending that increases its earning capacity Costs that maintain (rather than enhance) the asset's earning capacity
Includes Purchase cost, delivery, legal charges, installation, upgrades, replacements: all costs of bringing the asset to its present location and condition Repairs, maintenance, repainting, renewals
Frequency Usually one-off Regular, recurring
Benefit period Several accounting periods A relatively short period
Accounting Added to the asset's cost (statement of financial position), then depreciated Expensed in the income statement in the period incurred
The biscuit machine

A company buys a machine to make biscuits.

  • Purchase and installation = capital expenditure.
  • Later repairs and maintenance = revenue expenditure. They only maintain the machine; they don't increase what it can earn.
Quick test

Ask: does it create or improve an asset (more capacity, longer life, new capability), or is it the cost of acquiring and installing it? Then it's capital. Does it just keep the asset running as before? Then it's revenue.

2The general ledger

Definition

The general ledger is the complete record of financial transactions and events. It holds the account information needed for the financial statements, with accounts for assets, liabilities, capital, income and expenses.

Modern classification of ledger accounts:

  • Asset accounts: land, building, machinery, debtors, cash, bank
  • Liability accounts: creditors, bank loan
  • Capital account: capital, drawings
  • Income accounts: sales, rent income, discount received
  • Expense accounts: purchases, rent, wages, discount allowed

Posting from the primary books

  • Day-book totals go to the main accounts (e.g. sales journal total → Cr Sales). Individual lines go to personal accounts (each debtor Dr).
  • Cash-book entries go to the opposite side of the other account. A receipt on the cash book's debit side is credited to the paying account.
  • General journal entries are posted as stated.

3The trial balance

Definition

A trial balance is a list of the closing balances of ledger accounts on a certain date. It is the first step towards preparing financial statements.

  • Debit balances: assets, expenses, drawings.
  • Credit balances: liabilities, capital, income.
  • If everything is recorded and extracted correctly, total debits = total credits.

Purposes

  1. A working paper for preparing financial statements.
  2. Confirms that every debit has a matching credit (double entry).
  3. Confirms balances were accurately extracted from the ledger.
  4. Helps identify and correct errors before the statements are prepared. Fixing errors afterwards takes much longer.

Limitations

  • It only confirms that total debits equal total credits.
  • It can agree despite errors. An incorrect debit can be offset by an equal incorrect credit. A transaction never recorded at all leaves both sides missing, so the totals still agree.

4Worked example: posting to the ledger and extracting a TB

A small printing firm, May 2019

Transactions

Key ledger balances

  • Cash: 800 − 180 − 38 = 582 Dr
  • Bank: 2,200 + 340 − 174 − 610 − 230 = 1,526 Dr
  • Purchases: 610 + 214 + 174 = 998 Dr
  • Sales: 340 + 720 + 810 = 1,870 Cr
  • Rent: 180 + 230 = 410 Dr
  • J Shihan, M Tilan, J Wasantha: fully settled, so nil

Exam focus

The biggest exam task here is a full cycle:

  1. Opening balances
  2. Transactions
  3. Journals (including petty cash and trade/cash discounts)
  4. Ledger accounts
  5. Balance them
  6. Trial balance

Remember:

  • Trade discount is deducted before recording (e.g. 500,000 rice less 10% = 450,000).
  • Cash discounts go to the discount columns.
  • A vehicle donated by the owner is Dr Motor vehicle / Cr Capital (general journal).
  • The petty cash reimbursement equals the total spent.

Also expect capital vs revenue classification and TB purposes and limitations.
