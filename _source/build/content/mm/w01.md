## What multimedia means

:::key Definition
**Multimedia** combines digitally manipulated text, images, graphics, sound, animation and video, delivered through a computer or another electronic device. “Multi” means many. “Media” are the means used to communicate information.
:::

A **multimedia asset** is an individual content item: a photograph, sound recording, text passage or animation. A **multimedia project** brings assets together for a purpose. A **multimedia title** is the packaged software or presentation through which the audience receives that project. A title can be downloaded, delivered through a website or distributed on storage such as a DVD.

The lesson discusses desktop computers, phones and ubiquitous computing environments. Multimedia careers include graphic design, web design, animation, audio production, video production and project management. A developer needs both technical skills and an understanding of the message and audience.

| Element | What it contributes | Sri Lankan example |
|---|---|---|
| Text | Names, instructions, facts and abstract ideas | Tamil instructions in a railway ticket kiosk |
| Still image | A captured view of the real world | A photograph of Sigiriya |
| Graphics | Designed visual information | A bus-route diagram |
| Sound | Speech, music and effects | Spoken Sinhala directions |
| Animation | Designed changes over time | A moving diagram of a water pump |
| Video | Recorded moving pictures, often with audio | A demonstration of making tea |

**Virtual reality** tries to immerse a person in a simulated environment. **Augmented reality** adds digital information to a view of the physical world. Multimedia can stimulate sight and hearing, and a system with suitable hardware can also provide touch or haptic feedback. Ordinary multimedia does not require all three senses.

:::example A useful combination
A lesson about a solid's structure can combine a definition, a labelled diagram, a rotating 3D model and spoken explanation. Each element should help the learner understand the same concept. Adding unrelated music or animation can distract the learner.
:::

## Interactive media, hypertext and hypermedia

**Interactive multimedia** lets the user control what content appears and when it starts, stops or changes. The user's action changes the experience. In **non-interactive multimedia**, the audience follows the presentation's predetermined sequence without controlling its content flow.

| Type | Main feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Non-interactive | Content follows a fixed presentation | A cinema screening |
| Interactive | User input controls the experience | A learning application with selectable lessons |
| Hypertext | Text contains links to other information | A clickable term leading to its definition |
| Hypermedia | Linked information includes different media types | A museum map linking to photographs, audio and video |

Hypertext is not restricted to a single linear reading order. Hypermedia extends this linked structure beyond text. Interactivity and the number of media types are separate questions: a recorded video has several media elements even if its presentation is non-interactive.

:::warn Source wording
Section 1.2 describes multimedia as interactive use of “at least one” information type. Read that alongside section 1.1's combination definition and section 1.3's distinction between interactive and non-interactive media. Interactivity is a property of an application; it is not compulsory for every multimedia presentation.
:::

## Devices and the multimedia system

The lesson's “multimedia elements” section also describes the equipment needed to operate a system. Keep **content elements** separate from **hardware and software components** in an exam answer.

| Component | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Processing device | Encodes, decodes and transforms digital data | Computer or server |
| Input device | Receives the user's choices | Keyboard, mouse, joystick, touchscreen |
| Output device | Presents information to the senses | Screen, speaker, haptic device |
| Capturing device | Obtains assets from the real world | Camera or microphone |
| Storage device | Holds or distributes digital assets | Hard disk or DVD |
| Software and utilities | Create, coordinate or play the assets | Image editor, authoring program, media player |

A touchscreen can be both an input and an output device. A camera is an input source specifically used for capture. A single physical device may therefore have more than one role.

:::flow From capture to audience
Camera or microphone captures an asset
Computer processes and encodes it
Storage holds the asset
Network or storage medium distributes it
Application decodes and presents it
User interacts through an input device
:::

**Connecting devices** link equipment or interfaces: for example a USB connection between a microphone and a computer. **Communication devices** transfer data between systems: for example a network adapter, router or modem. Connecting a local device and communicating over a network are related but different jobs.

## Classifying assets: origin and time

The PDF uses two independent classification dimensions. First ask **where the information came from**. Then ask **whether its meaning depends on time**.

- **Captured media:** obtained from the real world. Examples are photographs, recorded video and recorded sound.
- **Synthesized media:** generated by a computer. Examples are text, designed graphics and computer animation.
- **Discrete media:** organised in space without a required time dimension. Also called static, non-time-based, non-temporal or space-based media.
- **Continuous media:** changes or unfolds over time. Also called dynamic, time-based or temporal media.

## Why multimedia is useful

The PDF lists six system properties or benefits. Explain the connection between each property and the user's task.

1. **Ease of use:** a clear application reduces effort, improves effectiveness and shortens training time.
2. **Intuitive interface:** a control's appearance suggests what it does. A recognisable house icon suggests returning home. It still needs clear labelling for unfamiliar users.
3. **Immersive experience:** the user concentrates on the application with fewer distractions. This can support attention and productive work.
4. **Self-paced interaction and retention:** a learner can pause, repeat and move forward at a suitable speed. Repetition can support remembering.
5. **Better understanding:** complementary words, pictures, animation and narration explain relationships that text alone may struggle to show.
6. **Cost effectiveness:** a clear digital message can reach many people and be updated for another version. This is a potential benefit, although initial production can be expensive.

:::example Education, entertainment and public services
An O/L science lesson lets students replay an animation and answer questions. A game combines graphics, sound and user control for entertainment. A tourism kiosk presents language choices, maps and narrated site information. A business uses product demonstrations for training and sales. A government service can explain a form using clear steps and spoken guidance.
:::

## Organising information

The content structure describes how screens or information units connect. Gather material for the purpose and audience, divide it into meaningful units, and choose a structure that makes those units easy to find.

| Structure | Navigation rule | Example and reason |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | Follow a sequence from one item to the next | A safety demonstration with ordered steps |
| Hierarchical | Follow branches of a tree | Website: home → subjects → lessons |
| Nonlinear | Follow links freely without one predetermined route | An encyclopedia with cross-references |
| Composite | Combine free navigation with constrained sequences | Choose a lesson freely, then follow its required demonstration |

## The three layers of a multimedia system

Figure 1.4 can be read from physical support upward to the user's application. Synchronisation spans the structure so that related elements occur at the right time.

:::grid
**Application layer**

Tools and applications, documents, user interfaces, programming abstractions and development methods. This layer lets people create and present projects.
+++
**System layer**

Operating systems, database systems and communication systems. These provide shared computing and networking services.
+++
**Device layer**

Storage, networking, compression and the processing of sound, images, graphics, video and animation. This layer supplies the basic mechanisms and physical-device support.
:::

:::example Follow one action
A student presses Play in a learning title: the **application** handles the command, the **system** retrieves the stored file, and **device-level** mechanisms decode and output picture and sound. Synchronisation keeps speech aligned with the relevant moving picture.
:::

## Development tools and new media

A multimedia system generates, represents, stores, transmits, searches, retrieves and delivers information. Select a tool for the operation and asset involved.

| Tool category | Main purpose |
|---|---|
| Music sequencing and notation | Arrange musical events and scores |
| Digital audio editing and synthesis | Record, trim, mix or generate sound |
| Graphics and image editing | Draw graphics and process photographs |
| Video editing | Select and arrange moving-picture clips and audio |
| Animation | Create changes in an object's properties over time |
| Multimedia authoring | Integrate assets, navigation, timing and user interaction |

**New media** are digital, computer-based forms with computational behaviour and opportunities for interaction. Examples in the PDF are blogs, social networks, digital games, virtual reality and online newspapers.

| Traditional mass media model | New-media model |
|---|---|
| Mainly one producer to many receivers | Many participants can produce and receive |
| Mainly one-way distribution | Feedback and interaction can shape content |
| Examples: broadcast radio, television, printed newspaper | Examples: blogs, social networks, interactive digital experiences |

Figure 1.5 shows a central source distributing outward in the first model and a network of connected participants in the second. The lesson describes the move from mainly passive Web 1.0 use toward participation associated with Web 2.0. Treat this as a communication-model comparison, not a claim that every online page is interactive or that broadcasting has disappeared.

## Challenges and evaluation

Multimedia development can be costly, difficult and time-consuming. Continuous assets require large storage and transmission bandwidth. Their timing constraints matter: late frames, interrupted playback or unsynchronised speech reduce quality. Automatically analysing, indexing and organising audio, images and video is harder than searching text. Effective systems need suitable algorithms, hardware and knowledge across several disciplines.

The PDF gives **two evaluation perspectives**. A functioning title is not automatically a useful title.

| User perspective | What to check |
|---|---|
| Subject and content | Is it relevant to my need? |
| Platform | Does it work on my hardware and software? |
| Usability | Can I understand and operate it? |
| Cost | Is the cost acceptable? |

| Developer perspective | What to check |
|---|---|
| Content | Are the subject and materials suitable and accurate? |
| Performance | Does the application operate efficiently with a good content flow? |
| Delivery | Is the distribution medium appropriate? |
| Interface | Does the interface fit the user and purpose? |

For example, a science lesson can combine a labelled diagram, narration and a replayable animation.”

## All six PDF review questions: model answers

### 1. What is multimedia? How does it vary from mass media? What are the salient features of new media?

Multimedia combines digital media elements in an electronic presentation. Mass media describes communication to a large audience, commonly through a one-to-many model. These terms describe different dimensions: a television programme can be both multimedia and mass media. New media are computer-based and support computation, interaction, feedback and many-to-many participation. A social learning forum lets students consume material and contribute explanations.

### 2. What are the challenges of an enhanced multimedia platform?

The system must store large assets, transmit them fast enough, respect playback timing, synchronise media, and process different formats. Searching and indexing image, audio and video content is difficult. Development requires efficient algorithms, capable hardware, specialist skills, time and money. A high-resolution lesson video may need compression and an appropriate delivery strategy to play smoothly on a limited connection.

### 3. Describe uses in education and entertainment.

In education, narrated demonstrations and animations explain processes, interactive exercises give feedback, and replay supports self-paced learning. In entertainment, films coordinate video and sound, games respond to player input, and virtual experiences immerse the audience. A Sri Lankan history lesson can connect a site map to photographs and spoken explanations; a game can combine animated characters with sound effects and choices.

### 4. What are the different categories of multimedia?

An application may be interactive or non-interactive. Its assets may be captured or synthesized, and discrete or continuous. Its information may be arranged linearly, hierarchically, nonlinearly or in a composite structure. State which classification dimension you are using and give examples; these categories do not form one mutually exclusive list.

### 5. Describe connecting, storage, input/output and communication devices.

Connecting devices and interfaces link equipment, such as USB connections for a microphone. Storage devices hold assets, such as a hard disk or DVD. Input devices receive user actions, such as a keyboard or touchscreen; output devices present content, such as a screen, speaker or haptic unit. Communication devices, such as a network adapter or router, exchange data between systems. A device may serve several roles.

### 6. Describe the classification of multimedia contents.

Use the origin-by-time table. A photograph is captured and discrete. Recorded speech and recorded video are captured and continuous. Typed text and a designed graphic are synthesized and discrete. Computer animation is synthesized and continuous. “Static” means not intrinsically time-based; it does not mean the file can never be edited. “Dynamic” means that the asset unfolds in time.
