## Direct vs indirect labour

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct labour cost** | Payments to **direct workers**: employees **directly involved in making the product**. Part of the **prime cost** | Machine operators, assemblers, tailors in a garment factory |
| **Indirect labour cost** | Payments to workers **not directly involved** in making the product. Part of **overheads** (Week 10) | Supervisors, storekeepers, maintenance staff, cleaners |

:::formula
**Prime cost = direct materials + direct labour + direct expenses**
:::

## Time keeping & time booking

**Time keeping** records **attendance** (arrival and departure). **Time booking** records **how the time was spent** (on which jobs).

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Manual**: attendance register, disc method | Workers sign in a register, or move a numbered disc/token |
| **Mechanical**: dial time records, **card time records** (clock cards) | A machine stamps arrival and departure times |
| **Traditional time cards** | Employees manually record arrival and departure on a card or register |
| **Biometric systems** | **Fingerprint or face recognition** verifies identity at clock-in and clock-out |
| **Access control systems** | **Proximity or swipe cards** tapped on entering or leaving |

**Time-booking documents:**

- **daily time sheets**
- **weekly time sheets**
- **job tickets or job cards**

:::key Job card
A **job card** keeps a **close check on the time an operator spends on each job** during the day. It records the job code, quantity, start and end times, and processes. It's the basis for charging **direct labour to jobs**.
:::

### Job evaluation
Job evaluation ranks jobs to set fair pay rates. There are three methods:

1. **Grading**
2. **Ranking**
3. **Points value**: points for **(a) education or background knowledge, (b) responsibility, (c) working conditions, (d) complexity of duties**

## Methods of remuneration

:::flow
Time rates
Piece rates
Time + piece combinations
Premium bonus schemes
:::

### 1. Time rates (day-rate system)

:::formula
**Wages = Hours worked × Rate per hour**   (plus **overtime** at a **premium** rate for hours over the basic day)
:::

:::example Lecture example: overtime
Basic rate **Rs 200 per hour**, **8 normal hours** a day, overtime at **1.5×**. The employee works **11 hours**.

| | Rs |
|---|---:|
| Normal hours: 200 × 8 | 1,600 |
| Overtime: 200 × 1.5 × (11 − 8) | 900 |
| **Total earnings for the day** | **2,500** |
:::

| **Advantages** | **Disadvantages** |
|---|---|
| **Simple** to understand and operate | Workers may **not care to improve efficiency** |
| Best when **quality matters more than quantity** (precision work), or when there's **no basis for paying by performance** | **Efficient workers' effort is not rewarded** |

### 2. Piece rates

:::formula
**Wages = Units produced × Rate per unit**
:::

| **Advantages** | **Disadvantage** |
|---|---|
| **Encourages productivity** and time efficiency; motivates workers to **produce more** | **Reduced quality**: workers may focus on **quantity over quality** |

### 3. Combination and 4. premium bonus schemes
**Bonus schemes** were introduced to **compensate time-paid workers** who otherwise **can't earn more by working more efficiently**. There are **individual** and **group** bonus schemes.

Two classic individual time-saved schemes (beyond the notes, but commonly examined):

:::formula
**Halsey:** Earnings = (Time taken × Rate) + **50% × Time saved × Rate**
**Rowan:** Earnings = (Time taken × Rate) + **(Time taken ÷ Time allowed) × Time saved × Rate**
:::

## The salary sheet, EPF & ETF

**Items in a salary sheet:**

- **basic salary**
- **fixed allowances** (e.g. cost-of-living allowance)
- **overtime**
- **EPF and ETF**
- **other deductions**, e.g. **loan instalments**

### Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)
- Set up under the **EPF Act No. 15 of 1958** (with amendments).

:::key EPF contributions
- **Employer: 12%** of the employee's monthly earnings
- **Employee: 8%** of monthly earnings (**deducted from salary**)
- **Monthly earnings for EPF = basic salary + fixed allowances − no-pay leave**

Overtime is generally **excluded** from EPF earnings.
:::

### Employees' Trust Fund (ETF)
- Set up under the **ETF Act No. 46 of 1980**.
- **The employer** contributes **3%** of the employee's total monthly earnings.
- **There's no employee deduction for ETF.**

:::tip Common mistakes
- **EPF and ETF are based on basic + fixed allowances**, not on overtime.
- **The 12% and 3% are not deducted from the employee**; they're **extra employer costs**.
- The **employee's 8%** *is* deducted from the salary.
:::

### Practice 09.1 — Work out overtime earnings

**Question:** An employee works 45 hours: 40 normal hours at Rs 200 per hour and 5 overtime hours at 1.5 times that rate. No other pay or deductions apply. Find gross earnings.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 09.1</summary>

1. Normal pay = 40 × 200 = **Rs 8,000**.
2. Overtime hourly rate = 200 × 1.5 = **Rs 300**.
3. Overtime pay = 5 × 300 = **Rs 1,500**.
4. Gross earnings = 8,000 + 1,500 = **Rs 9,500**.

**Check:** 45 × 200 = 9,000 includes ordinary pay for the overtime hours; add only the premium 5 × 100 = 500 to reach the same 9,500. Do not pay the same hours twice.

</details>

### Practice 09.2 — Build a small salary sheet

**Question:** For this exercise only, basic pay is Rs 40,000, fixed allowance Rs 5,000 and overtime Rs 3,000. The supplied contribution base excludes overtime. Assume employee EPF 8%, employer EPF 12%, employer ETF 3%, a Rs 2,000 employee loan deduction and no other deductions. Find net pay and total employer cost.

<details class="beginner-answer" markdown="1">
<summary>Show step-by-step answer — Practice 09.2</summary>

1. Contribution base = basic 40,000 + fixed allowance 5,000 = **45,000**.
2. Gross pay = 45,000 + overtime 3,000 = **48,000**.
3. Employee EPF = 45,000 × 8% = **3,600**.
4. Net pay = gross 48,000 − employee EPF 3,600 − loan deduction 2,000 = **Rs 42,400**.
5. Employer EPF = 45,000 × 12% = **5,400**. Employer ETF = 45,000 × 3% = **1,350**.
6. Total employer employment cost = gross pay 48,000 + employer EPF 5,400 + employer ETF 1,350 = **Rs 54,750**.

**Check:** net pay 42,400 + employee EPF 3,600 + loan recovery 2,000 = gross pay 48,000. Employer contributions are not deducted from the employee’s pay. Loan recovery is not a reduction of salary expense.

</details>
