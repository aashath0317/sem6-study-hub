1What is a budget?

Definitions
  • Budget: a detailed plan, expressed in quantitative terms, that specifies how an organisation will acquire and use resources during a particular period. In the lecture's words, "a quantifiable action plan for a specified time period" 01:19.
  • Budgetary control: a system of planning and controlling cost.
  • Budgets flow from strategic planning. Every enterprise has goals: profitability, growth, public service.
  • Strategy aims at achieving and maintaining a competitive advantage through critical success factors: the key strengths most responsible for the organisation's success.
  • A budget is built on past activities: last year's figures plus planned changes 02:29.

Purposes & functions of budgeting 03:51

Purpose Meaning
Planning Plan annual operations: what and how much to produce and sell
Facilitating communication & coordination Departments share information, giving harmony between departments
Allocating resources Scarce resources go where they're needed
Managing financial & operating performance (control) Compare progress against the budget; take corrective action
Evaluating performance & providing incentives Motivates managers towards organisational goals

Steps in budgetary control

  1. Establish budgets
  1. Measure actual performance
  1. Compare actual with budget
  1. Analyse causes of variances & report

Sales forecasting: the starting point

The sales budget drives everything else. The factors are:

  1. past sales levels and trends
  2. general economic trends
  3. economic trends in the industry
  4. other factors expected to affect industry sales
  5. political and legal events
  6. intended pricing policy
  7. planned advertising and promotion
  8. expected actions of competitors
  9. new products planned by the company or others
  10. market research studies

3The functional budgets: formats

Sales budget: Sales units × Selling price = Sales revenue
Production (units) = Budgeted sales + Closing finished goods − Opening finished goods
Material usage = Production units × Material per unit
Material purchases = Usage + Closing raw materials − Opening raw materials (× cost per unit)
Labour budget = Production units × Hours per unit × Rate per hour

Why subtract opening and add closing? Opening inventory is already made, so we don't produce it again. Closing inventory must also be made this period 13:53.

5The cash budget: rules

Closing cash = Opening cash + Cash inflows − Cash outflows
  • It's prepared weekly or monthly so management can deal with shortages (arrange overdrafts or loans) or surpluses (invest them).
  • Only actual cash receipts and payments are included.
  • Depreciation is always excluded: it's not a cash flow.
  • Credit sales and purchases still outstanding at period end are excluded. Include cash when it's received or paid, e.g. a 30-day credit sale in March is received in April.
  • The closing balance of period 1 = the opening balance of period 2.

6Limitations of budgeting 36:08

  • Based on estimates and assumptions, so they lack accuracy.
  • Preparation takes time, cost and management effort.
  • Lack of flexibility: once fixed, budgets aren't changed for unexpected events (e.g. a COVID lockdown). This can demotivate employees who see the targets as unachievable 37:33.
  • Budgets can encourage budgetary slack and "spend it or lose it" behaviour.
